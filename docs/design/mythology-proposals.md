# Mythology Proposals

Three original directions for why the stairs exist. They share a setting, a protagonist and the silence mechanic.
See `docs/research/stairs-in-the-woods.md` for the research and the **DO NOT USE** list.

---

## Shared foundation

### Names

| Role | Proposal | Alternates | Collision check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park | **Sallow Branch National Park** ("sallow" is a willow; "branch" is the Appalachian word for a creek) | Pruett Gap NP, Low Kettle NP | A web search found no game, film or novel using it. Low risk. |
| Trail system | **The Cordwood Trails** (a CCC-built network of loops and spurs: Cordwood Loop, Pruett Spur, Tallow Creek Trail, Lookout Cutoff) | The Relief Trails, Hatchet Run | No known IP. "Cordwood" names a building technique and some small businesses, which is harmless. |
| Rental cabins | **Tallow Creek Cabins** (six cabins, off-season) | — | No known IP. |
| Ranger station | **Fork Station** | — | Generic. |
| Protagonist | **Ruth Ambler** | Jo Pell, Nell Aubrey | "Ambler" is a common surname, a Pennsylvania town and the author Eric Ambler. No game protagonist found. Low risk. **Avoid "Wren"** (heavily used in games), **"Carrow"** (Harry Potter) and **"Voss"** (used in many games). |

The names were checked with light web searches only. Run a trademark and Steam search before anything goes public.

### Protagonist premise
Ruth Ambler is a contract acoustic technician for the park's soundscape monitoring program. The program is modelled on the real NPS practice of leaving recording stations in the backcountry for 30 days. It is late October, and the cabins are closing for the season. Four stations along the Cordwood Trails sent back data that the lab calls impossible. There are hours of readings *below the recorder's own electrical noise floor*: the machines logged less sound than a sealed box would. Ruth's job is to hike out, pull the stations, check the hardware and write it up as a fault. She rents a cabin at Tallow Creek for the week.

This gives her a reason to go off-trail, a reason to listen, and a gadget (a handheld sound level meter and recorder) that can show the silence mechanic on screen. None of it uses the rescuer or official-witness framing.

Each proposal below adds a one-line personal hook.

### The silence mechanic (shared)
- `SilenceZone` nodes drain the nature category buses in a fixed order as the player gets closer. **Each mythology gives that order a meaning.**
- Ruth's meter shows a dB reading, which gives the player a readable "warmer and colder" signal without any HUD arrow.
- At the centre of a zone, the `Unnatural` bus fades in. Each mythology defines what plays there.

## A. The Returned Houses

**Core idea:** The land is rebuilding the homes that were taken from it, and it starts with the stairs.

### What the stairs are
When Sallow Branch was established in 1936, the state condemned the hollow's homesteads, and the houses were burned so nobody could come back. The land remembers those houses and is putting them back from the inside out. It starts with the stairs, the part of a house that is walked on most and so the part it remembers best. Each stair stands where a real homesite stood. It is always the *indoor* stair: painted treads worn pale in the middle, a newel post with a missing cap, dry under rain.

### Why the forest goes silent
Inside a house, you don't hear birds. The silence is the house going up around you: walls you can't see, a roof you can't see. Animals avoid it the way they avoid a building.
- **Bus order has meaning.** `Distant` goes first (the door shuts on the world), then `Birds`, then `Insects`, then `Wind` (the walls are up), and `Water` last (the plumbing).
- **`Unnatural` is interior room tone:** a clock tick, a stove settling, a floorboard taking weight somewhere upstairs where there is no upstairs.
- **Zone radius is the house's footprint.** Players can learn to walk the walls.

### How the rules change across acts
| Act | Rule |
|---|---|
| Arrival | An ordinary park. Real CCC steps and homesite chimneys are everywhere, and the player learns to walk past them. |
| Exploration | Stations show silent readings. Some foundations have *too-clean* stone steps, which are deniable. |
| First Staircase | The first full indoor stair stands on a homesite. It is silent, with room tone. Nothing happens, which is the point. |
| Forest Changes | More stairs. Then door frames, as gaps between trunks exactly a door wide. Trail markers bend to follow old property lines. |
| Park's History | The 1936 condemnation files: family names, tract numbers, "structure burned" stamps. Each stair matches a tract. |
| Reality Unstable | Room tone follows Ruth outside the zones. Her rental cabin is built on a tract, and one night it has one more stair than yesterday. |
| Revelation | The Tallow Creek Cabins stand on the most valuable tract. The land isn't haunting the park. It is **moving back in**, and the cabins are in the way. |
| Ending | A complete house stands in the woods, lit, silent, with the door open. Ruth goes in, or leaves it and hears the forest come back one bus at a time. |

### What the documents reveal
- **Condemnation ledger** (Fork Station archive): tract numbers, owners, appraisals, "burned per order". One tract has no owner name, only "resident declined to state".
- **Cabin guest books:** decades of cheerful entries. Some guests mention "the neighbours' house through the trees", and there is no neighbour.
- **Ranger maintenance tickets:** "Removed unauthorized steps, tract 41. Again."
- **Station data sheets:** Ruth's own logs, where quiet hours line up with tract boundaries.

### Ambiguity kept
The 1936 survey and the 1937 burn list disagree about whether a house ever stood on the final tract. Either the land is returning a home, or it is building one that was never there, for someone who hasn't arrived yet.

### Cheap environmental beats
1. A lone stone chimney with a fresh mud dauber nest on the *inside* of the flue, as if the room were warm.
2. A doormat half-buried in leaf litter at the foot of a stair.
3. A stair with a child's height marks pencilled on the stringer, dated after 1936.
4. Two trunks exactly a door-width apart, with a worn path through the gap and no path on either side.
5. A cabin guest book entry thanking "the lady next door" for pie.
6. A tract-number survey pin in the ground at the foot of every stair.
7. Room tone recorded on Ruth's recorder at a station where she heard nothing.
8. The rental cabin's staircase with one tread in a different, older paint.

**Personal hook:** Ruth's great-grandmother's maiden name is on the condemnation ledger. Ruth doesn't know it, and the player may notice before she does.

## B. The Held Breath

**Core idea:** The forest is re-living the minute before a fire that killed a work crew, and the stairs are the way out it keeps offering too late.

### What the stairs are
In 1939, a relief-work crew building the Lookout Cutoff was caught by a wind-driven fire in a steep drainage. They died trying to reach the lookout tower. The tower was later taken down, and only its footings remain. Since then, stairs rise in the woods: plain, sturdy tower flights, pine treads, galvanized bolts, always pointing uphill toward where the lookout was. The forest is building the tower's stairs where the men needed them.

### Why the forest goes silent
The silence is evacuation. Before a crown fire, wildlife leaves. What the player hears is the gap where the animals *just were*. The silence is a warning the forest keeps giving, for a fire that isn't there.
- **Bus order has meaning.** `Birds` first (they fly), then `Insects`, then `Water` (it thins as though evaporating), with `Wind` kept and *rising*. `Distant` gets replaced.
- **`Unnatural` is a low, far-off roar,** like a train with no rails nearby, plus the tick of heating metal.
- **Zone behaviour:** zones move uphill slowly over time, so standing still lets the silence pass over you.

### How the rules change across acts
| Act | Rule |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Burn scars on old trunks and a memorial plaque at the trailhead are just scenery. |
| Exploration | Station data shows silences at the same minute each day. Ash sits on fresh snow or rain puddles. |
| First Staircase | A lookout flight rises out of a draw, bolted to nothing. The silence and a faint hot-metal tick. |
| Forest Changes | The light goes orange-grey at the silent minute. Leaves fall *up*. Stairs start connecting into switchbacks. |
| Park's History | Crew roster, fire report, lookout logbook. The lookout's last entries describe the smoke "holding still". |
| Reality Unstable | The silent minute lengthens. The cabin clocks stop at it. Ruth hears the crew's tools as a rhythm on `Unnatural`, never as voices. |
| Revelation | The stairs lead to where the tower was, and the tower is there during the silence. The lookout never saw the fire in time because the forest was silent *for him too*, and he took it for calm. |
| Ending | The fire arrives, or never arrives. Ruth either climbs and sounds the alarm or walks out as the birds come back. |

### What the documents reveal
- **Lookout logbook:** weather entries, rotting pages, and the last day's entries all written at the same minute.
- **CCC-style crew roster** with a column of blank "discharged" dates.
- **Fire report:** a bureaucratic finding of "no negligence found" beside a hand-drawn map with stairs sketched where none were built.
- **Guest books:** guests over the years complain of a "burning smell, no fire" and "birds all left at once, then came back".

### Ambiguity kept
Is the forest remembering a fire, or warning about one to come? The final station reading carries a date stamp from next summer.

### Cheap environmental beats
1. Scorch marks on the uphill side of trunks only.
2. A dented canteen hanging from a bolt on the lowest stair tread.
3. Ash drifting up, done by reversing the particle system.
4. A trailhead plaque listing crew names, with one name scratched out and re-engraved.
5. The lookout footings with fresh, unweathered bolt holes.
6. A cabin smoke detector chirping only during the silent minute.
7. A tool rhythm (shovel, axe) that stays in time with Ruth's footsteps.

**Personal hook:** Ruth's grandfather was a fire lookout elsewhere and quit after one season without saying why.

## C. The Unheard

**Core idea:** Something in Sallow Branch removes things by making them silent first, and stairs are what it leaves behind.

### What the stairs are
Anything that stops making sound, stops being. The park has been losing structures for decades: a shelter, an outhouse, a whole cabin. It happens quietly and off the maps. Staircases are the leftovers because stairs are the part of a building that makes noise only when someone is *coming*. The erasure can't finish a thing that is waiting for footsteps. Every stair in the woods marks a building that no longer exists and has left no record.

### Why the forest goes silent
The silence is the erasure itself, spreading. It isn't sound being blocked. It is sound being *taken*. This is the story where Ruth's impossible station readings make literal sense: the recorders logged less sound than their own circuits made, because part of the recorder was being unheard too.
- **Bus order has meaning.** The order is random per zone. The erasure takes whatever is loudest first, so the player learns to read what is *left*.
- **`Unnatural` is nothing.** Instead, Ruth's own sounds fade: footsteps first, then breath. Sound returning to the player is the win state.
- **The meter** drops below 0 and keeps dropping, which is the one HUD lie in the game.

### How the rules change across acts
| Act | Rule |
|---|---|
| Arrival | The station at the trailhead reads normal. The park map has a few trail spurs that end in nothing. |
| Exploration | Faulty stations. Ruth replays recordings with gaps her footsteps should have filled. |
| First Staircase | A stair where a spur ends. Inside the zone, Ruth's footsteps make no sound. |
| Forest Changes | Things vanish while the player isn't watching: a bench, a sign, a trail marker. Only the ones that went silent first. |
| Park's History | Ranger inventories that shrink every year. Structures are delisted with no removal work orders. |
| Reality Unstable | Guest book entries lose their names. Cabins at Tallow Creek go quiet one by one, and then there are five cabins. Then four. |
| Revelation | Ruth's *own* station logs from week one describe her arriving a day she doesn't remember. The erasure has been working on her. |
| Ending | Ruth makes noise to stay (shouting, playing back her recordings), or goes quiet and lets herself be finished. |

### What the documents reveal
- **Asset inventories** from year to year with lines missing and no deletion notes.
- **Guest books** with signatures whose ink turns faint, then disappears, a page at a time as the game goes on.
- **Station calibration sheets** in Ruth's own handwriting that she has no memory of filling in.
- **Ranger radio log:** the transcript of a call in which the second speaker's lines are blank.

### Ambiguity kept
The ending never confirms whether anything was erased or whether Ruth is losing her hearing and her memory. The last scene is shown with *no* audio at all.

### Cheap environmental beats
1. A bear box with nothing in it and a padlock that's still locked.
2. A trail sign bolted to a post, reading an arrow and a mileage with no destination.
3. A guest book where one name per page is a faint indentation with no ink.
4. A cabin number sequence that skips a number, and the key hook board that doesn't.
5. The player's footsteps cutting out on a stair's first tread.
6. A photo on the ranger station corkboard showing a building behind the rangers that isn't there now.
7. A playback of Ruth's recording from a spot she stood at, with silence where she spoke.

**Personal hook:** Ruth has mild tinnitus. Inside silence zones it stops, and that is the only time she has peace.

## Comparison

| | A. Returned Houses | B. Held Breath | C. Unheard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theme | Displacement, belonging, grief | Premonition, duty, being too late | Erasure, memory, being forgotten |
| Real-world anchor | Strong: park land condemnations | Strong: CCC-era fire crews and lookouts | Weak: acoustic monitoring only |
| Meaning of silence | You are indoors | Everything has fled | Sound is being taken |
| Documents | Very natural (ledgers, deeds) | Natural (logs, reports) | Clever but abstract |
| Cost | Lowest: stairs, chimneys, room tone | Medium: fire, light and particle work | Low art cost, high sound and UI design cost |
| Risk | Could feel slow | Fire VFX might look cheap, and the story overlaps real tragedies | Bus-order-as-random is hard to read; "memory loss twist" is familiar |

## Recommendation: A. The Returned Houses

- **It is the most real.** Park land takings, burned homesteads and front steps to nothing are all documented history. That is the "it almost makes sense" feeling the research points to, and it sits far from every source on the DO NOT USE list.
- **Its silence means something specific.** "The silence is being indoors" is an original idea, and it gives the `Unnatural` bus a distinct identity (interior room tone) that costs almost nothing to make.
- **It suits the documents and places we already plan to build.** Cabins, ranger archives and guest books all carry the story naturally.
- **Its escalation stays restrained.** Stairs, then door frames, then walls, then a house. Things get stranger without gore, which is where the source falls apart.
- **The build is cheapest.** It needs stone, painted wood and audio loops, which suits the PS2 aesthetic.
- **Borrow from the others:** C's "readings below the noise floor" as the opening hook (already in the shared premise), and B's burn history as the way the 1936 houses were destroyed.
