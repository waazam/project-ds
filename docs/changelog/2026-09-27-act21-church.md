# 2026-09-27 — Act 21: the long stair, and the church in the snow

Commit: this entry's commit on `main`. Built from the owner's "The Real Act 21" document, their
cathedral and crypt references, their feedback on the first look ("more gothic and less white; the
glare hurts"), and their plan of the interior.

## The long stair (`LongStair`)
- The room at the top of the round room's shaft now opens onto one long straight staircase to the
  surface:
  - 52 flights of 32 steps with a landing between each, about 300 m of climb;
  - about three minutes at a sprint (stamina included), about five at a walk.
- It starts as bare poured concrete with caged bulbs and a steel rail. From about a quarter of the
  way up, timber appears: a shoring frame, a patch of plank lagging, a board laid over a tread. Over
  the middle of the climb, very slowly, the wood takes over.
- The last stretch is an old wooden stair between plank walls, lit by oil lanterns on the frames,
  with a wooden rail.
- The underground black fog swallows it a few flights ahead. A cold draught moans down it, and
  boards creak.
- At the top: a low wooden landing, iron rungs up its end wall, and a hatch in the ceiling.
  - "Push the hatch open": it swings up, and the player climbs through into the church's crypt.
  - It drops shut with a boom behind them, and from above it won't lift ("Shut fast").
  - This is a new save: `Act21ChurchReached`.

## Winter
- From the end of Act 20 it is winter everywhere:
  - every tree in the world frosts over through a new global shader value `winter`: snow lies along
    the upward faces of trunks and boughs, and a grey hoarfrost covers the rest;
  - the atmosphere has a winter mode (`ForestAtmosphere.Winter`): a pale cold overcast, a thin
    blue-grey haze, a weak white sun.
- The church stands alone in its own snowy glade (`WinterGlade`):
  - a rolling snowfield;
  - a ring of snow-laden firs, grey dead snags, and a new kind of tree: leafless, forking into a fine
    crown of twigs, with snow along its limbs;
  - snow falling in four great sheets round the church, never inside it.
- Softer footsteps: a new `snow` footstep surface (muffled press, dry squeak) for the snow. The
  glade uses it, and so does a drift blown in through a broken aisle window.
- The compass comes back to life: its marker points at whatever the church wants next (the nearest
  unlit candle, then the vestry's key, the font, the great door).

## The church (`Church`, `Church.Shell`, `Church.Interior`, `Church.Gothic`, `Church.Crypt`, `Church.Story`)
- **The biggest house yet**, a Gothic cathedral:
  - a seven-bay nave 16 m wide between clustered piers, with pointed arcades into the side aisles;
  - a triforium of little arches;
  - paired stained-glass clerestory lancets;
  - a ribbed vault 36 m up (tiercerons, a ridge rib, gilded bosses), the tallest ceiling in the game.
- **The crossing and the transepts**: a proper groin where the vaults meet. At the end of the left
  arm is the vestry's door; at the end of the right, the chapel's.
- **The chancel and apse**: up five steps, choir stalls either side, the high altar under a gilded
  canopy (four columns, gables, pinnacles, a spire).
- **The west end**:
  - the great door, iron-banded oak, with four niches cut into it; gold outlines at their backs show
    a chalice, a bell, a crown and a dove;
  - the rose window over the door;
  - the font by the door, its lid chained and padlocked;
  - the tower's small locked door.
- **The crypt**:
  - brick groin vaults on squat stone columns with cushion capitals, over black and white marble;
  - stone tombs, an old altar draped in red, a little pipe organ, a hooded stone figure;
  - twin stairs up into the nave through balustraded openings in its floor.
- **The owner's plan and the gothic pass** (after "too white, it hurts"):
  - The stone, floor, vaults and crypt are all a good deal darker and sooted. There is a dark
    interior haze and little ambient light. The vaults and walls now block the sun (they were
    letting it straight in).
  - The church is lit by candles: iron racks of votives in an alcove under every aisle window, an
    iron crown of candles hanging low over a round dais at the crossing, and altars in both transept
    arms.
  - The left transept's altar is bloodied, and the blood is dragged across the floor to the vestry's
    door.
  - Five small chapels open off the apse (the owner's plan). Each has a step up, a narrow vault, its
    own stained lancet, a stone figure, and a candle before it.
  - Round the high altar, a great circle of red light is worked into the floor.
  - The only cold light is the snow-light through the windows. The stained glass glows against the
    dark. A faint warm glow high along the triforium lets the vault's ribs read in the dark.
- **Textures** (`ChurchTextures`, all through the texel-density upscaler):
  - limestone ashlar, vault plaster, cream and red marble, the medallion;
  - crypt brick and the chequered floor;
  - dark oak, old planks, gilding, iron and snow;
  - three families of stained lancets, the rose, clear leaded quarries, and the red sigil.
- **Sound**:
  - a cavernous reverb in the nave;
  - a new winter wind loop round the building and a new low church room tone;
  - creaks, chains and moans high up;
  - the forest's own ambience silenced inside.

## The owner's second round (after playing it)
- A little brighter overall: more ambient light inside, and the candles glow brighter and further.
- A moulded base course along the foot of every wall (the owner's castle-arch reference): a plain
  plinth standing out from the wall, a sloped weathering on top, a roll moulding under it, and a
  fillet at the foot. It stops at every doorway. It runs round the aisles, the west front, the
  transepts, the chancel, the apse (not across the chapels' openings), the vestry and the crypt.
- The floor is now cobbles: rounded stones of every size and shade of grey and brown, worn smooth
  on top, packed in dark grit. The red runner, the medallion and the crypt's chequer stay.

## Act 21's puzzle (the first of the great door's four pieces)
1. The great door is locked, with four empty niches. The font's lid is chained and locked.
2. Four tall standing candles stand round the nave, two by the door and two before the crossing.
   Light each with the lighter.
3. When all four burn, a heavy lock turns far off and the vestry's door (left transept) groans open
   by itself.
4. In the vestry (a wardrobe, a table with a candle, a window onto the snow) the font's iron key
   hangs on the hooks.
5. The sexton's note on the table tells where the four pieces went. It sets up Act 22: the bell
   "where bells go", the crown "with the dead", the dove "to the Lady".
6. The key unlocks the font. The lid lifts off and falls aside, and in the black water stands a
   silver chalice.
7. The chalice goes into its niche in the great door and settles there with a soft glow:
   "One of four."
8. That is Act 21's end (`Act21Finished`). The credits follow, with the polaroid montage (moved here
   from Act 20's end).
