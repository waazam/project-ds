# Pacing and tension map (alpha, Acts 1–21)

How the build breathes. Every act is rated for stress, from 1 (quiet, safe) to 5 (a chase, a fight
or a scare that can kill), and given a role. The rule we keep: never more than two 4–5s back to back
without a quiet beat (a 1–2) after them, so the player has somewhere to put their fear down before
it's picked up again. Scare fatigue sets in when the dread never drops.

## What this pass changed (the owner's pacing and tension mapping)
- **The sewer (Act 17)** sits between the hallway and closet (15–16) and the boss (18). That was
  four hard acts in a row. Its haunting sounds now come every 16–34 s (they were every 8–20 s), so
  it reads as a wet, uneasy crossing rather than another spike. The fight gets its full weight after
  it.
- **The station's basement (Act 13)**: 14–30 s between haunts (was 9–22). It's a puzzle space; the
  dead eye's slaps carry the fear there.
- **The long stair (Act 21)**: 18–34 s between its moans and creaks (was 10–22). Over a
  three-minute climb they were becoming wallpaper. Now each one lands.
- **The church (Act 21)**: 30–56 s between haunts high in the vault (was 18–36). It's the quiet
  puzzle act; the scares are the shut hatch and the blood, not a noise every few seconds.
- The quiet acts (3, 12's opening, 14, 19, 20, 21) are left quiet on purpose. Nothing was added to
  them.

## Readability in the dark (the lighting pass's other half)
Every door the player needs, or needs to know is locked, can be seen without the lantern:
- the great door has a candle on a bracket either side;
- the vestry's and the chapel's doors each have two;
- the tower's door has one;
- the hatch at the top of the long stair has a thin cold light round its edges;
- the crypt's stairs have the nave's light spilling down them;
- the four puzzle candles stand out as the only dark ones among dozens burning.
