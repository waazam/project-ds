# Project DS: Story

The authoritative outline of the game's story, act by act. Implementation follows this document. If the game and this document disagree, this document wins; raise the difference with Dan before changing either.

**Revision 2 (2026-09-22): the linear story.** Dan asked for a story with no backtracking. Act 1 happens on the Blackfern Trail. The first climb leaves the player unconscious, and they wake somewhere else entirely: the Hollow, a new stretch of deep woods. Everything from Act 2's end to the credits happens there, along one route that only goes forward: a stranger's camp, the cabin, the footbridge, the clearing of stairs, a lookout over the cabin, the bunker, and the last staircase.

**R.H.** The player is alone. The man whose things they keep finding is a stranger, a hiker the stairs took a day or two before them, known only by his initials, R.H. He is never seen. His entries in the trail register, his notes, his photographs and what he left behind tell his part, and the player is following his trail without meaning to.

**Two levels.** The trailhead level holds Act 1 and the first climb. The Hollow level holds everything after. The climb's blackout carries the player from one to the other.

---

**Voices.** Every voice in the game (the clearing's voice, the whispers, the choir, the radio) is deep, never high-pitched.

---

## Act 1: Vacation
**Checkpoint 1**, at the trailhead.

Nothing should be overtly scaring the player yet except for the atmosphere in the game. We want the player to feel as if they are being watched, but not from the beginning of their exploration. It needs to gradually feed into the player's subconscious that they are being watched. Nothing overtly can be seen by the player yet, just implied. The audio and atmosphere carry the eeriness.

The real horror and scares only start happening after the player finds the stairs. Yet these scares still need to feel like they aren't real at first. The player should be thinking that they are just seeing things due to their imagination of how creepy the woods is, but stuff is really getting weird.

**Arrival.** The player arrives at the Blackfern trailhead parking lot on a bright afternoon. It is sunny and clear at the lot, and the fog only closes in as the trail goes deeper into the forest. The trail register, the park's bird card and the signs are there to read for anyone who looks. The last two entries in the register are the same hiker's, R.H.: "Blackfern, the old steps, back by dark" on the 24th, and just "the steps" on the 25th.

**Opening.** The game opens over black with two short lines saying where the player is (Overlook Park, the Blackfern Trailhead, late October) and what they are doing (a day of hiking, with a camera for whatever they find on the way). Then it fades in on the player at the open back of their car in the lot, taking the camera out of the trunk.

**Photos.** The player carries a camera and can take pictures of anything on the way: birds, a deer, a frog by the creek, wildflowers, mushrooms, the waterfall, a strange standing stone, or just the trees. Nothing is required and there is no checklist. Every picture goes into the player's album (Tab), a simple collection of the photos they have taken. It is all optional, there to add realism: it's what someone could be doing on a trip to the woods before things kick off with the stairs.

- There are only 3 birds in the game (cardinals in red, blue and purple), because the photography becomes irrelevant once the player finds the stairs.
- There is a 4th bird, black with a red eye glow. Taking a picture of it triggers a distant but harsh, loud scream, and the birds all disappear.
- A photo of the stairs comes out wrong.
- The camera disappears once the player takes their first step on the stairs; from that step there is no way back down.

**The stalker** does not appear on the Blackfern Trail at all. The feeling of being watched in Act 1 comes from the audio and atmosphere only. It starts following the player in the Hollow.

**Finding the stairs.** The trail ends at a fallen fir. Past it, old stone pavers set into the ground mark the way to the stairs, close enough together to follow. At the top of the stairs stands a stone newel post, the thick pier that ends the stair wall, made of the same stone as the staircase. Its round cap has been broken off; only a jagged stump is left. The ground to the left of the fallen tree is blocked, so nobody tries to go around that side. Where the stones start, the player thinks: (nothing is said) (The register's last entries, R.H.'s "the old steps" and "the steps", are the same way.)

## Act 2: Stare
Triggered when the player finds the stairs for the first time.

When the player gets within a radius of the stairs, all of the ambient audio from the forest goes dead silent. A low hum, barely audible, emanates from the stairs, growing the closer the player gets.

Stepping on the stairs makes the game scarier in a less subtle way than not stepping on them. Stepping on them is a trigger for more horror than the subtlety of just seeing them.

When the player steps on the first step:
- The camera is gone. They climb the stairs themselves, at their own pace, but the stairs will not let them back down: turn round and the way down is simply not there any more.
- At the top landing stands the stone newel post with its cap broken off. Inspecting the stump (E) is the trigger (nothing is said).
- Their vision falls shut and drifts open again, each blink deeper than the last, until it is all black. The player passes out on the landing.

**Waking.** The player comes to face down somewhere in the middle of the woods, in the dark, in a place they have never been: the Hollow. It is night, and it stays night: everything from here to the end happens on this one night (the only daylight in the game was the trail before the fog). It is raining: before their eyes open they hear it, the patter on the leaves, low thunder somewhere off. Their eyes open and fall shut before they stay open, and their sight clears slowly as they lift their head. The camera is gone. **Checkpoint 2.**

Nothing is said. A little way ahead, a lantern is burning at someone's camp.

## Act 3: Homebase
**Checkpoint 3**, on reaching R.H.'s camp.

R.H.'s camp: his tent, a cold fire ring, his pack, and on a stump the lantern and the compass with a note from him, written to whoever finds it: *"If you're reading this, it got you too."* He was going up the hollow to an old cabin for the night, and the needle knows the way. Past it, he writes, there is a bunker with a steel door and four numbers. From this note on the HUD keeps a count of the code ("CODE _ _ _ _"). The player thinks: (nothing is said)

**Compass.** Equipping it points to the player's current objective, but it gradually goes haywire the closer you get to the stairs. It is an unobtrusive little UI element at the very top of the screen, similar to Skyrim's compass but a bit more translucent, with N, S, E, W shown accurately. It reads N, S, E, W normally until quests mess with it or the stairs get too near. When the objective changes, the marker never snaps or swings quickly; it sweeps to the new heading over a few seconds. Fast direction changes are disorienting and not fun. From the camp on, the compass points to the cabin.

**Leaving the safe zone.** After picking these up, the moment you step outside the camp's radius (the "safe zone"), the game goes dead silent under the rain, as if you were by the stairs. The rain that has fallen since the wake goes on, low thunder rumbles, and lightning crackles across the sky, distant but bright.

**Lantern.** A radial light source. Its beam can be focused by holding right-click, like the other focus mechanic.

All we hear is the patter of the rain while we follow the compass toward the cabin, following the man who wrote the note. Beside the path on the way, two of R.H.'s notes are nailed to trees at eye height, a number written big on each: looking at one up close reads it, lantern or no lantern (the number flashes up on screen), and the HUD keeps the digits found.

## Act 4: The Storm Walk
On the way to the cabin, in the storm, nothing big shows itself: this walk is the stalker's introduction as a character. Within seconds of the lantern and compass being taken it is behind a tree, and from then on it never lets up until the footbridge: its clicking rattle from wherever it stands, and its footsteps answering the player's a beat behind, from behind, from either side, from ahead, as it moves from tree to tree around them, so the sounds swing round. It never makes any other sound: no growl, no snarl, no screech. Every glimpse is the last of that glimpse: caught, it is simply gone, silently. It feels like it is constantly watching, and it cannot be looked at. (The giant is not seen here; only its far footfalls reach the walk. It is saved for the lookout in Act 7.) The compass keeps pointing to the cabin.

## Act 5: Let Me In
**Checkpoint 4**, on getting inside the cabin.

An old cabin stands beside the path in the hollow. Its door has been completely boarded up, from the outside. This is the breaking-into-the-cabin puzzle. The compass points directly at the cabin from every angle as the player walks around it.

**Tools.** Tools lie around for the player to pick up. The player can carry any number of tools, and every item they carry works at any time without selecting it. A tool is "used up" when it's used correctly in the puzzle.
- **Axe:** round the far side of the cabin, stuck in the chopping block beside the woodpile, no glint: the player has to walk round the cabin to find it. With it, the player can cut the wood planks barricading the front door to get in.
- **Hammer:** in the open shed beside the cabin. With it you can pry the nails out of the front door. It's a little minigame, so it takes longer to get in than the axe.

**Inside.** R.H. is not there. What is there tells the story: his chair pushed back from the table, a roll of bandage and dark stains, his photographs pinned to the wall (the three birds, and the stairs at night) and his last page on the table, signed R.H. The page says the cap came away in his hand at the top of the stairs, that his hand hasn't been his since that he didn't board the door, not to take the cap anywhere, and: *"If you get in and I'm gone, I went up."* On the table in front of his chair is a newel post bulb: the missing stone cap from the top of the stairs, with the same broken neck. The moment the player steps into the room, something pounds on the outside wall beside the table: three heavy blows that shake the view, and right after them the cabin door slams shut behind them on its own, the lantern guttering; E opens it again. The rain falls silent while they are inside. When they pick up the cap, the compass points in a new direction (across the footbridge).

When they step outside with the post, the rain stops at that moment. Nothing is said: the player never talks to himself (Dan, 2026-09-22).

## Act 6: Mourning
**Checkpoint 5**, on crossing the footbridge.

**Outside.** Stepping outside, the rain has stopped. Still night, fog thick between the trees.

**The forest turns menacing.** After the newel post quest triggers, the whole forest shifts into a more menacing tone. The trees are noticeably thicker and taller, casting more shadows. The deeper you go, the ground turns veiny: first something that could be worms, then an interconnected, pulsating veiny mesh on the ground.

**The clearing of stairs.** Once the player crosses the footbridge, the compass points them to a small clearing. The first staircase stands in the middle of it, the same one from the end of the Blackfern Trail, surrounded by 15 small variations of staircases: spiral stairs, short stairs, wood stairs, concrete stairs, stairs sliced in half, burned stairs, all kinds of variations. This is a key horror moment.
- When the player steps into the clearing's radius, a voice says clearly, but as if from a dreamlike distance: *"Come up and see"*. A red spotlight finds the player.
- Then the clearing answers: the stairs' hum swells for a few seconds and the voice comes from three directions at once (*"Come up and see"* and its echo). Nothing is taken: the player keeps carrying the cap, and the clearing's staircase stays broken.
**One way in.** The clearing is closed by a ring of deadfall from the moment it appears, with one gap: the trail the player walks in by. They can turn round and go back out that way, but the trail onward cannot be walked from inside. The only way forward is the stairs. The clearing is thick with fog; the far staircases are grey shapes in it. One of the whole staircases stands right in the middle of the trail where it enters, its foot toward the player.

**The loop.** "Stuck walking up different staircases before eventually falling off", continuously up, never down. The voice has been calling *"come up and see"* out of the trees ahead since the moment the footbridge was crossed, and keeps calling into the clearing and until the fall. Stepping onto ANY staircase in the clearing, from any side, starts the loop: the ten whole flights, the five broken stubs and the original in the middle (a stub or the original hands the player straight on to a whole flight in the fog). Nothing is lit until the player chooses; the whole staircases are long (about fifty steps) and all much the same size. From then on the light and the compass follow the flight they are on, and every flight is one-way: once they have started up it they cannot back down, whichever way they face.
1. They climb to the top of the flight they chose. As they reach the landing the fog closes in around them to a near white-out, the view drifts, the hum rises, and with no cut, no flash and no stop they are part way up *another* long staircase, facing up it, still walking; the fog thins and the new flight is there in front of them.
2. Sometimes a hand-off puts them at the flight's foot, more often part way up it, a third or half way, so it feels like being stuck on stairs rather than reaching bottoms and tops. Four to six flights in all (random), each a different one, the voice calling at every hand-off, and blood beginning to rain, a few drops at first and heavy by the last flight. It should feel fluid and trippy, one long walk, never beats skipped. This is the only place the voice calls and calls: it belongs to the stairs, and it is never heard on the trail or in Act 1.
3. At the top of the last flight the landing gives way: a slow stumble off the back, the view tipping down, the dark coming in over the last of the drop, a soft thump. The blood rain stops with the fall.
4. They wake on the trail past the clearing, the same night, in fog so thick the trees are grey. Nothing is said. Behind them the stairs, the ring and the clearing's own flight are simply gone; the meadow stands empty and the trails are as they were. The compass points up to the ridge above the creek.

## Act 7: Safety
**The fire.** The path climbs the ridge above the creek to a lookout, the compass leading. When the player wakes the cabin is already burning across the water; the moment it comes into view is **Checkpoint 6**, and the giant crosses far beyond it. Nothing is said. The trail then runs on along the near bank of the creek, right past the burning cabin across the water (the fire lights the bank as they pass; the cabin itself is out of reach and its door is sealed by the fire), and the compass leads on to the bunker without ever turning back toward the clearing.

**Der Riese.** A beat after that, far beyond the burning cabin, a GIANT version of the stalker walks past behind the far tree line. The gap across the creek is just enough to see only its neck and head moving through the tops of the far trees, to add scale and dread. Not a sound from it but the soft thud of its stride, felt more than heard, about twelve seconds, and it is gone, never seen again until the last staircase. The player keeps control the whole time; it is a glimpse while they stand looking at the fire.

**No whispers on the walk.** The voice is only ever heard where staircases stand (the clearing behind them, the last staircase ahead); the walk to the bunker is silent of it.

**The bunker.** The compass now points onward to a new structure, a bunker deep in the woods beyond the ridge. Two more of his notes hang on trees beside the path on the way; their numbers complete the code. At the bunker the dial on the door is pre-filled with the digits found, and the four in order open it.

## Act 8: The Bunker
**Checkpoint 7**, when entering the bunker.

Inside, the player is in a long hallway with overhead lights lining the way forward. The hallway is almost an optical illusion in the way it seems to stretch on forever.

**The lights.** The further the player walks down the hallway, the white LED lights start to flicker, then eventually transition to a deep, unsettling fluorescent red. If the player turns around after the red-light trigger, all the lights behind them back to the entrance change to red too, to add to the feeling of the player's sanity slipping. Part way down, once the lamps have begun to fail, they all die at once and the creature is standing right in front of the player, lunging at their face, and is gone the same instant the lamps snap back red.

**The end.** At the end of the hallway, the clean industrial bunker floor gives way to an overgrown, viny door that is barely cracked open, waiting for the player to push it open (E to interact) and walk inside.

## Act 9: Belly of the Beast
When the player goes through the overgrown door, they see a room filled with old CRT TVs stacked on top of each other, like that scene in The Matrix, showing mass surveillance of the woods. The station's log lies open on the console. The moment they are through, the vine door swings shut behind them on its own; it stays locked ("It won't move.") until the screens are done and the dead walkie-talkie is taken off the console: pushing it before then only rattles it.

**The screens.**
1. The compass points to one specific screen showing the cabin smouldering.
2. An interact prompt appears to turn it off. When the player presses E and turns off that TV, they all turn off.
3. After 5 seconds they all turn back on, showing one image in unison. It starts fuzzy, then slowly clears until it is all the stairs in the woods.

The walkie-talkie lies on the console beside the log, dead: no light, no sound. The player takes it (E); nothing happens. The compass then points to the way out (the bunker's entrance). The way out will not change until the walkie-talkie is in hand.

## Act 10: Escaping the Bunker
With the screens done and the walkie-talkie in hand, the shut door gives: pushing it open again (E, or walking into it), the long straight hallway is gone. They are in a bare concrete room with three doors, and the door they came in by shut behind them.

**The choir.** *"Come and see"* is chanted in unison by a choir of unsteady, wavering voices. (Dan can record audio for this if it can't be made to work.)

**The rooms.** Open any door and the player is standing in the same room again, the door behind them shut: the same lamp, the same stains, the same three doors. Deep lighting colour changes, the feeling of something following. This repeats a random number of times (two to four).

**It.** Then the room they step into has the creature in it, hanging upside down from the ceiling, facing them. The scare is the old walkie-talkie burst, the super loud static, with its roar under it. Then the lamp dies: the room is completely black and all that can be seen are its two glowing eyes. The doors ahead slam shut. The door behind the player swings open and they are shoved through it, out into a long hall with the bunker's own round front door at its far end and the night beyond; the door slams shut at their back. The only way is down the hall. As they run, its clicking rattle grows louder and quicker behind them, and heavy footfalls follow: glance aside and there is nothing there; but turn back toward the door at all and, once, it is right there in front of their face for a blink, then gone. All the way to the round door.

**The walkie-talkie.** The player bursts out through the bunker's original front entrance. Outside, in the night in front of the hatch, the dead walkie-talkie in their hand crackles to life. That is **Checkpoint 8**.

## Act 11: The Third Man
**The radio.** Once you pick up the radio, a static-y voice begins to ask you questions for the rest of the game.
- *"Did you see them?"* the radio scoffs out.
- *"Who are you!"* the player says.
- Static. Radio silence follows.

The compass points onward, deeper into the woods beyond the bunker, to the stairs: the first staircase is standing there again. On the walk the radio asks *"Are you alone out there?"*

**The climb.** This time the stairs are far bigger and go impossibly high up into the trees, their top lost in the fog. The player climbs them on their own feet, no pull: it takes minutes, and there is no way back down (a couple of treads up, the way behind is closed). The hum rises with every step until it is very loud at the top. The stairs themselves say *"come up and see"* only once or twice: once from somewhere up in the fog as the player comes under them, and once about half way up. The radio keeps at them the whole way: *"Don't go up."* as they come under the stairs, then on the climb *"Who's there with you?"*, *"Don't. Don't go up there."*, *"Who's that behind you?"* and, near the top, *"Is someone with you?"*

**The top.** On the top landing the broken newel post is right there, in reach at last, and the cap the player has carried since the cabin goes back on it (E, "Put the cap back"; empty-handed the stump only reads "The broken post."). It leaves their hands, turns, and grinds down onto the stump, stone on stone, with a purple flash at the joint. The hum surges, Only this staircase is ever made whole. From here the player has no control again.
1. The treetops are covered in thick fog. The player has no control from here.
2. The giant monster that crossed the far trees in Act 7 is standing on the landing in front of the player, so close and so tall that they have to look up, and up, its body to its head.
3. The radio asks *"Do you see him now?"* The player doesn't recognise it until it opens its eyes: the giant's great glowing red eyes stare down at the player, piercing with their gaze. It makes no sound: the hum swells and swells.
4. The hum is so loud now. The player is trembling, looking it in the eyes, and it leans in over them.
5. Their eyes fall shut and drift open again, each blink deeper than the last, its eyes the last thing they see, until it is all black. They pass out looking it in the eyes.

**The end.** Black. The hum, at its loudest, dies away to silence. Over black the radio asks one last time: *"Did you see them?"* Then the credits: the end card, "GLHFDD", "Thanks for playing.", and the main menu. There is no waking.
