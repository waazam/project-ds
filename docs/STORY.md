# Project DS: Story

**This is the master story document.** It is the game's story, act by act, and implementation follows it. It is updated every time Dan makes a decision or the story changes, in the same commit as the change. If the game and this document disagree, this document wins; raise the difference with Dan before changing either.

**Revision 5 (2026-10-16): the revised outline adopted.** Dan's revised outline (the "fourth pass" of 2026-10-15, *Dead Silent: Revised full story outline*) replaces Revision 4 as the story, with his decisions on where it met the game. What changed:
- **Who the player is.** Cameron Webber, a young photographer, in late October 1996, shooting an assignment for a magazine. His father, Mitchell Webber, was the chief ranger at the forester station and died there in the winter of 1987 to 1988.
- **The opening.** A drawn map of the drive, the assignment and a page on the pager replace the two black cards.
- **Act 1 is longer.** A cabin by the lot is its hub: three walks out and back, and a backlit photo board that shows the wrongness. Voices lead the player to the stairs on the last walk.
- **The title.** DEAD SILENT plays after the first climb, over the load into the Hollow. The second chapter's title plays on the first step.
- **The cabin.** It is one cabin all game: the Act 1 hub, boarded in the Hollow in Act 5, burning around the player in Act 7, and standing burned in Act 25.
- **Act 7 (the hybrid).** The player walks on to the cabin, is trapped by the board's fire, and breaks out with the axe (a seventh death, "Burned Alive"). Falling trees herd them up to the lookout, where the giant still paces its sky lane over the burning cabin. The walk from the lookout to the bunker stays open.
- **The player's voice.** A short list of lines the player says, shown as text for now and recorded later.
- **The stalker.** It is the one enemy in many masks, and the wendigo wears Mitchell.
- **The end.** The Act 11 giant throws the player to the lake. The Act 25 reveal, and the figure in every Polaroid after it.
- **Kept.** The key in two halves (Revision 4.2) stays as built.

**Where it happens.** The game has two levels.
- **The trailhead level** holds the opening, Act 1 and the first climb. At the end it holds Act 25 as well: the way back to the car, past the burned cabin.
- **The Hollow level** holds everything between, Acts 2 to 24, along one route that only goes forward:
  - a stranger's camp, the cabin, the footbridge and the clearing of stairs;
  - the cabin again, the lookout, the bunker and the last staircase;
  - then, far from the woods, the lake, the forester station and down under the world;
  - up into a winter church, the winter woods, a ski lodge and the snow under it.

The climb's blackout carries the player from the trailhead into the Hollow, and the wendigo's burn-through carries them back.

**Time.** Act 1 runs from a sunny afternoon (the first walk), into a late afternoon with the fog rising (the second), into dusk and thick fog (the third). The Hollow's woods are one night, from the wake to the giant (Acts 2 to 11). The player wakes at the lake at sunrise. From the end of Act 20 it is winter everywhere: grey snow light, dusk on the road, noon behind the snow in the lodge, and night in the storm. Act 25 is the autumn woods again.

**Chapters.** The story is told in eight chapters: *Back to Nature*, *The Stairs*, *Root of the Problem*, *Dawn is Breaking*, *Ever Upward*, *The Great Door*, *Enjoy Your Stay* and *Return*. Each opens with its title alone, big over the picture, with no chapter number.

**R.H.** The player is alone. The man whose things they keep finding is a stranger, a hiker the stairs took a day or two before them. He is known only by his initials, R.H., and he is never seen. He is trying to get whoever comes after him safely through. He is not the father. These tell his part:
- his entries in the trail register;
- his note at the camp, his key cut in two, and his page and photographs in the cabin;
- his boots in the bunker, his initials inked inside them;
- six pages torn from his notebook along the way (the lake shore, the station door, Room 3, the sewer's landing, the church crypt, the lodge by Room 203);
- a missing poster in the lodge's walls: a missing hiker, 41, last seen at the Blackfern trailhead on October 25 (the day of his last register entry), a green pack and a camera round his neck, his name torn off and "the steps" pencilled over it.

The player is following his trail without meaning to.

## Rules

- **Voices.** Every voice in the game is deep, never high-pitched: the woods' voices, the clearing's voice, the whispers, the choir, the radio, the wendigo's stolen voices, and the player's own lines when they are recorded.
- **The player's lines.** The player says a few short lines aloud, like Ethan Winters, and only these (the list is in "The player's lines" below). Until Dan records them they are shown as text: one line under the picture, in quotes, in the player's own subtitle style (not the hint style), for about as long as it would take to say. When recorded, they are dry and unfiltered (not through the radio). Besides them, a rare thought caption of one or two words stays (like "Trenches." and "So quiet down here."). Nothing else is said to himself.
- **The stairs** get no backstory. They are never explained.
- **R.H.** is never seen, and the stalker never wears him.
- **Photos.** There is no checklist. The album shows a count of how many of the 70 things have been found, with the points ("N/70 found   N pts"), and the credits say "N of 70" over "N points", but nothing ever lists what is left to find.
- **Sound.** No sound repeats: many variations, never the same one twice running.
- **Visuals.** Nothing flashes or strobes, and the camera never shakes hard or snaps. One exception (Dan, 2026-10-16): the black bird's scream in Act 1 jolts the camera as it hits.
- **Titles.** Chapter titles are shown alone, with no chapter number.

## Opening: The Drive
Morning. Chapter one: *Back to Nature* comes later, after the camera.

The game opens on a parchment map, drawn like the map travel in Indiana Jones. A red dotted line draws itself from the magazine's office to the Blackfern Trailhead in Overlook Park, and a small car icon travels along it. Over the map, typed like a sheet from the office, the assignment: *[magazine TBD]: Autumnal Delights*, the October feature, a nature photographer's trip into the woods. As the car nears the trailhead, a pager's small green screen comes up at a corner of the picture with a page from the editor: "Take your time up there. It's been a rough month." It cuts out mid-glow, as if the signal died. Nothing more is said about it; the loss is carried by the photo in the cabin and by what he chooses to photograph.

The map dissolves straight to the trunk (there is no drive scene). This replaces the two black cards.

He chose the trailhead because he loved it as a child. The magazine wanted nature shots. He is not looking for his father.

## Act 1: Back to Nature
**Checkpoint 1**, at the trailhead.

**The trunk.** He has parked in the lot: sunlight, wind. At the open back of the car he takes the camera out of the trunk. Then the camera's how-to line (right mouse raises it, left mouse shoots, Tab opens the album), then the first chapter's title.

**Arrival.** It is sunny and clear at the lot. The trail register, the park's bird card and the painted park signs are there to read for anyone who looks. The last two entries in the register are the same hiker's, R.H.: "Blackfern, the old steps, back by dark" on the 24th, and just "the steps" on the 25th. Nobody has signed since. This is the first seed.

**Photos.** The player carries a Polaroid-style instant camera, and keeps it for the whole game. Right mouse raises it, left mouse takes the picture and Tab opens the album.
- **Subjects.** They can take pictures of anything: birds, a doe, a frog by the creek, wildflowers, mushrooms, the waterfall, a strange standing stone, or just the trees.
- **The album.** Every picture goes into the album, scored for clarity, focus, framing and zoom (one to five stars). The camera zooms (x1 to x4), focuses itself, and fires a soft flash in the dark.
- **No checklist.** The album's footer keeps count of how many of the 70 things have been photographed, and the points ("N/70 found   N pts"), but never lists what's left.
- **The player's notes.** After the prints, the album has a page of pencil notes in the photographer's own hand, a line for each thing the first time it's on film.
- There are only 3 birds in the game (a northern cardinal, an indigo bunting and a purple finch, as on the park bird card; Dan, 2026-10-16). They are gone from Act 2 on.
- A 4th bird, black with red eyes, is on the third walk (below).
- Photographed, a bird flies off through the trees and is gone only once it is out of sight.

### The cabin
A small old cabin stands beside the lot, rented for the shoot. It is the hub of Act 1, the only warm, safe place in it, and it is the same cabin the player will find boarded in the Hollow (Act 5), burning (Act 7) and burned (Act 25). It is one model, the same inside and out wherever it stands.
- **Inside.** Warm light in the window. On the table: the assignment sheet (its themes, not a list of subjects: autumn light, wildlife, a place that matters) and a framed photo of two people, a man and a boy, the man's face turned away or out of the frame. On the wall: a backlit photo board, empty and glowing.
- **The board.** Standing at it, E pins the player's photos: the best-scored prints taken since the last visit go up (up to eight, the highest stars first), filling the board. It is a display, never a list of what to take. The first visit has no pictures yet ("Nothing to pin yet." as a hint), so he goes out. The door stays open behind him as he leaves.
- **The walks.** Act 1 is three walks, each a loop out from the cabin and back to it, then out along the third and never back. Pinning at the board after a walk moves the day on: the light and fog go to the next walk's, and the next way out opens. (Before, the next walk's way is not open: an old trunk lies across it. The walks share the trail where they overlap.)

**The photo board.** What the board shows over Act 1:
1. After walk 1: the pictures as taken, in good light.
2. After walk 1, looked at again: in one frame, small and at the edge of the trees, something tall and black that wasn't there. A second frame is slightly blurred in the middle where nothing moved. Pin again (E) and look: the black shape is gone from the picture. The hum is faintly there, only in this room.
3. After walk 2: the falls print comes out surreal and old, faded like a photograph decades old, its colour gone, and a figure stands on the rock where he once stood with someone. The two-person photo on the table has changed the same way.
4. After walk 2, looked at again: the black shape is back, closer, in a frame he took facing the other way.
5. Last: a picture he does not remember taking, the stairs, shot from the cabin window.

The black shape is only ever in the prints. It is never in the woods in Act 1.

### Walk 1: The Photo Trip
Afternoon, clear. A normal walk in golden light, the best the woods will ever look: up the trail, over the footbridge, out to the overlook on its hill and back down the ridge path. The player photographs anything; the first of the three birds (the cardinal) is on this walk. Nothing is wrong. The only hints are sound: a distant twig snap, a bird that stops mid-song, the distant rifle. Back at the cabin he pins his best shots to the board (stages 1 and 2).

### Walk 2: The Place That Mattered
Late afternoon, the fog rising. The assignment asked for a place that matters. The way leads to the falls and the rock over them, the old favourite spot where he came as a child: off the trail before the footbridge, along the creek and up beside the falls, then down the hillside to the trail by the mile sign. The last of the three birds (the purple finch) sits by the rock. Mist on the water; the falls are seen in full. He shoots it for the person he lost.
- **The voice.** On the way back, one word in the woods, close and ahead of him: *"come."* Deep, felt more than heard. Turning to it, there is nothing. It is said once.
- Back at the board: stages 3, 4 and 5.

### Walk 3: Come and See
Dusk, thick fog. The cabin's light is gone behind him once he is a little way out; there is no going back to it.
- **The black bird.** The fourth bird, black with red eyes, sits on a branch, its eyes catching the light. Photographing it brings a distant but harsh, loud scream: every bird vanishes, and it lunges straight at the player as it goes. Its print comes out dark, with its eyes in it.
- **The voices.** Now they are plain: *"come and see"*, deep, many of them, from the trees and from behind. If he follows them they lead on toward the stairs. If he doesn't, they lead on anyway: fallen trunks close the other paths, each falling as the walls do below (creaking first, falling only in view). The voices stop at the stairs.

### The woods close in
Near the trail's end a leaning board says TRAIL CLOSED / No Entry Past This Point. A dead fir stands at the end of the trail. There is no path past it: the woods make one, and the player sees it happen.
- **Seen.** Every tree that comes down ahead of them creaks first, and then waits, leaning and groaning, until the player is looking at it; only then does it crack and fall. They fall flat and heavy, never floating or rolling, landing with a heavy thud.
- **The fir.** It starts to creak as they come near the trail's end, and comes down across the trail, tearing its roots up, once they are close and have it in view.
- **The stop.** The crack stops them: their legs go, as if startled, and their eyes are drawn (gently, never held) to the falling fir and then to the first trees of the walls coming down after it, one by one. It holds them seven to nine seconds to watch the falls; then they can walk again.
- **If they never look.** The fir doesn't wait for ever: if the player never turns to it, it comes down anyway after about twenty-five seconds.
- **The walls.** After that the trees either side of the old way come down as the player nears them, each in view, a wall on the left and then the right. Behind them, once they are past, more come down across the way they came, heard more than seen. None comes down within reach of the player.
- **The woods wait for the player.** If they wander off, turn back or stand still, nothing goes on without them: the walls come down only while the player is on the way and looking, and never with the player on the wrong side of one. A player who strays off the way into the woods has a tree come down across their path in front of them, in view, and they pull up to watch it land. A player who comes at the way from the side, or goes round the standing fir, starts the walls all the same.
- The fog keeps thickening all the way to the stairs. The way on is the only way, and it leads to the stairs, which he did not know were here. The register's "the steps" is now a place.

At the top of the stairs stands a stone newel post, the thick pier that ends the stair wall, made of the same stone as the staircase. Its round cap has been broken off; only a jagged stump is left.

## Act 2: Stare
Triggered when the player finds the stairs. Chapter two: *The Stairs*.

**The silence.** When the player gets within a radius of the stairs, all of the ambient audio goes dead silent (every sound, until Act 2's wake). A low hum, barely audible, emanates from the stairs, growing the closer the player gets. There is no game title here.

Stepping on the stairs makes the game scarier in a less subtle way than not stepping on them.

When the player steps on the first step:
- The second chapter's title, *The Stairs*, comes up alone over the picture.
- The stairs lift them off their feet. They glide up to the top landing on their own, silently, with no footsteps and no say in it. The mouse stays free to look around the whole way up.
- The stairs will not let them back down: turn round and the way down is simply not there any more.
- At the top landing stands the stone newel post with its cap broken off. Inspecting the stump (E) is the trigger (nothing is said).
- Their vision falls shut and drifts open again, each blink deeper than the last, until it is all black. The player passes out on the landing.

**The title.** In the black, the climb goes on: wooden steps slowing, breathing with them, the hum, a heartbeat far down. This black is the loading screen into the Hollow. The game's own name, **DEAD SILENT**, comes up over it and hides the load; then it clears.

**Waking.** The player comes to face down somewhere in the middle of the woods, in the dark, in a place they have never been: the Hollow. It is night, and it stays night through the Hollow's woods. It is raining: before their eyes open they hear it, the patter on the leaves, low thunder somewhere off. Their sight comes up slowly out of the black, once (no blinking back into it), and clears as they lift their head. **Checkpoint 2.**

As they get up, the player's line: *"Where the fuck am I? ... Was I... floating? Up those... stairs?"* A little way ahead, a lantern is burning at someone's camp.

## Act 3: Homebase
**Checkpoint 3**, on reaching R.H.'s camp.

R.H.'s camp: his tent, a cold fire ring and his pack. On a stump are the lantern and the compass, with a note from him written to whoever finds it:

> *"If you're reading this, it got you too. Don't stay out here after dark. There's an old cabin up the hollow. The needle knows the way. Past it there's a bunker. Steel door. I had its key. I cut it in two so it can't carry it in whole, and hid the halves off the path, one before the cabin, one before the bunker. Arrows on the trees take you to each, in the ink only the lantern's black light shows. Look close. Not every mark out there is mine. Leave the lantern lit. - R.H."*

**The key in two halves.** R.H. cut the bunker's key in two and hid the halves off the path, each at the end of a trail of arrows brushed on dead trunks in the blacklight's ink. The trails are the same every game:
- **Half 1** is off the walk from the camp to the cabin (Acts 3-4), the first stretch with the lantern. Its trail leaves the path a little before halfway and ends at a hollow stump; the half lies in a rusted tobacco tin on the rot inside it.
- **Half 2** is off the walk from the lookout to the bunker (Act 7), about halfway. Its trail ends at a cairn; the tin is tucked under the flat stone over a gap at its foot.
- **The arrows.** Each trail is seven dead trees, 11 to 15 m apart, and runs some 90 to 120 m into the woods. It is never a straight line: it makes two or three real turns of 35 to 60 degrees. Each arrow faces someone coming from the tree before and points at the next tree, or at the hiding place from the last one. The ink fades past about 6 m and is gone at 13, so the next arrow has to be looked for in the direction the last one points.
- **The trace.** Under the flame a trail is only dead trees. The one sign in plain light is on the first tree of each, a few metres off the path: an old, dried smear of the paint and a knife notch in the bark.
- **Decoys.** Two trees by each start and one by each end carry marks that aren't his: clawed hands, smears, tallies, an eye, struck-out digits. The ground round each start is marked too.
- **The hiding place.** Under the blacklight the tin glints from a few metres away; under the flame it is an old tin. One press opens it, the half shows in it, and it is taken, with the player's line "Half of a key." (or "The other half of the key."). It is saved at once. Each half found turns the stalker's clicking up a notch.

There is no tracker on the HUD; the halves are simply carried. At the bunker they are joined and turned in the door's keyhole (Act 7). Old saves carry over: a code digit from either walk counts as that walk's half, and a door already open had both halves, joined.

**Lantern.** A radial light source:
- F lights it and puts it out.
- Its beam can be gathered by holding right mouse, like the other focus mechanic.
- B switches to its **blacklight**, a steady violet cone that helps little to see by. It shows what is written in ink only it can show, lying on the bark, boards and stone it was put on: words, hands and footprints all over the game. A person's hand prints as a hand; the thing's prints are clawed.

**The flame gutters out.** A few seconds after the lantern is first lit at the camp, the flame gutters out: "The flame's gone out. B: try its blacklight." It won't take again until the blacklight has been on a while and has shown what's written round the camp. Then the wick catches again ("The wick's caught again. B: back to the flame."). It happens once. Once it is over, standing by the first tree of either arrow trail (the old paint on it) with only the flame on, before that half is found, brings one short line under the picture that names the blacklight's key ("Old paint on that tree.   B: the blacklight."). It comes once in a game, and the hints name the keys as the player has bound them.

**Compass.** Equipping it points to the player's current objective, but it gradually goes haywire the closer you get to the stairs.
- It is an unobtrusive little UI element at the very top of the screen, similar to Skyrim's compass but a bit more translucent, with N, S, E, W shown accurately.
- It reads N, S, E, W normally until quests mess with it or the stairs get too near.
- When the objective changes, the marker never snaps or swings quickly; it sweeps to the new heading. Fast direction changes are disorienting and not fun.
- On the move the compass fades out of sight. It comes back when the player stands still, when there is a new objective, and while it is going wrong near the stairs.
- From the camp on, the compass points to the cabin.

**Leaving the safe zone.** After picking these up, the moment you step outside the camp's radius (the "safe zone"), the game goes dead silent under the rain, as if you were by the stairs. The rain that has fallen since the wake goes on, low thunder rumbles, and lightning crackles across the sky, distant but bright.

All we hear is the patter of the rain while we follow the compass toward the cabin, following the man who wrote the note.
- A little before halfway, the first arrow trail leaves the path (see "The key in two halves" above). It runs off through the trees to the hollow stump that holds the first half.
- Round its start stand dead trees with marks that aren't his: handprints (most of them clawed), smears, tallies, an eye, struck-out digits. The ground is marked too, here and now and then along the whole walk.
- Under the flame an arrow tree is just a dead tree. Only the blacklight on it, from close by, shows the arrow.

## Act 4: The Storm Walk
On the way to the cabin, in the storm, nothing big shows itself: this walk is the stalker's introduction as a character. This gaunt, hunched black figure is its true form, and every later monster is a mask over it (see "Lore").
- **Heard first.** From the camp until well over half way to the cabin, it is only heard: its clicking rattle from wherever it stands, and its footsteps answering the player's a beat behind, on the same mud and leaves, heavier and a few dB under them, stopping when they stop. The sounds come from behind, from either side and from ahead, as it moves from tree to tree around them, so they swing round.
- **Then seen.** After that it is behind a tree, a sliver of shoulder and head leaning out, one long hand on the bark. It moves only while it is outside the player's view. From then on it never lets up until the footbridge.
- **Its only sounds.** It never makes any other sound: no growl, no snarl, no screech.
- **Glimpses.** Every glimpse is the last of that glimpse: caught, it is simply gone, silently. Now and then it holds the player's eye a moment longer before it slides back behind its trunk. Far ahead at the fog's edge it sometimes just stands watching, and goes only if they come on.
- **The dark.** Leave the lantern off out there and it comes on faster and closer.
- **The birds.** When it takes a tree nearby, a few birds sometimes go up out of the canopy over it.
- It never enters the silence round the stairs, and it withdraws indoors.

It feels like it is constantly watching, and it cannot be looked at.

Now and then the dark makes a sound of its own, never seen and never the same twice:
- a branch coming down far off;
- something stepping in the mud alongside that stops when they stop;
- a trunk groaning with no wind;
- something heavy dropping from a tree behind them;
- a twig snapping close behind;
- three knocks on wood.

(The giant is not seen here; it is saved for Act 7.) The first half of R.H.'s key lies off this walk at the end of his arrows (Act 3), seen only by the blacklight. The compass keeps pointing to the cabin.

## Act 5: Let Me In
**Checkpoint 4**, on getting inside the cabin.

A cabin stands beside the path in the hollow, and it is the cabin from Act 1, by the lot, in the wrong place: the same walls, the same window, the same door. Its door has been completely boarded up, from the outside. This is the breaking-into-the-cabin puzzle. The compass points directly at the cabin from every angle as the player walks around it.

**Tools.** Tools lie around for the player to pick up. The player can carry any number of tools, and every item they carry works at any time without selecting it. A tool is "used up" when it's used correctly in the puzzle.
- **Axe:** round the far side of the cabin, stuck in the chopping block beside the woodpile, no glint: the player has to walk round the cabin to find it. With it, the player can cut the wood planks barricading the front door to get in.
- **Hammer:** in the open shed beside the cabin (its door shut until E opens it). With it you can pry the boards loose from the front door. It's a long hold, so it takes longer to get in than the axe.

**Inside.** R.H. is not there. What is there tells the story: his chair pushed back from the table, a roll of bandage and dark stains, and his last page on the table, signed R.H. The player's own photo board from Act 1 hangs on the wall, with R.H.'s photographs pinned across it (the three birds, and the stairs at night). The assignment sheet and the two-person photo lie on the table beside the cap. A second axe hangs on pegs over the hearth: it is only dressing here (it can't be taken), planted for Act 7.

> *"It came away in my hand at the top like it wanted to. The cap off the post. The hand hasn't been mine since. I kept the rest of me. The door won't open from in here. I didn't do that. Don't take it back up. Don't take it anywhere. If you get in and I'm gone, I went up."*

On the table in front of his chair is a newel post bulb: the missing stone cap from the top of the stairs, with the same broken neck. The moment the player steps into the room, something pounds on the outside wall beside the table: three heavy blows that shake the view. Right after them the cabin door slams shut behind them on its own, the lantern guttering; E opens it again. The rain is shut out while they are inside. When they pick up the cap, the compass points in a new direction (across the footbridge), and the storm breaks there and then, for good: the rain stops while they are still inside the cabin, and the drips off the roof and the boughs die away about twelve seconds later. The moment they open the slammed door to leave with the cap, the clearing beyond the creek (its stairs, its ring of deadfall) and the blood rain come into the world; none of it is seen from the cabin.

When they step outside with the cap, the rain is already over and the woods are still. Nothing is said.

## Act 6: Mourning
**Checkpoint 5**, on crossing the footbridge.

**Outside.** The rain stopped while they were still in the cabin. Still night, fog thick between the trees. The blood rain is already falling ahead, unseen by the cabin: it comes in over the walk to the footbridge and is all there once the creek is crossed.

**The forest turns menacing.** After the newel post quest triggers, the whole forest shifts into a more menacing tone. The trees are noticeably thicker and taller, casting more shadows. The deeper you go, the ground turns veiny: first something that could be worms, then an interconnected, pulsating veiny mesh on the ground.

**The clearing of stairs.** Once the player crosses the footbridge, the compass points them to a small clearing. The first staircase stands in the middle of it, the same one from the end of the Blackfern Trail, surrounded by 15 small variations of staircases: spiral stairs, short stairs, wood stairs, concrete stairs, stairs sliced in half, burned stairs, all kinds of variations. This is a key horror moment.
- When the player steps into the clearing's radius, a voice says clearly, but as if from a dreamlike distance: *"Come up and see"*. A red spotlight finds the player.
- Then the clearing answers: the stairs' hum swells for a few seconds and the voice comes from three directions at once (*"Come up and see"* and its echo). Nothing is taken: the player keeps carrying the cap, and the clearing's staircase stays broken.

**One way in.** The clearing is closed by a ring of deadfall from the moment it appears, with one gap: the trail the player walks in by. They can turn round and go back out that way, but the trail onward cannot be walked from inside. The only way forward is the stairs. The clearing is thick with fog; the far staircases are grey shapes in it. One of the whole staircases stands right in the middle of the trail where it enters, its foot toward the player.

**The loop.** "Stuck walking up different staircases before eventually falling off", continuously up, never down.
- **The voice.** It has been calling *"come up and see"* out of the trees ahead since the moment the footbridge was crossed. It keeps calling into the clearing and until the fall.
- **Starting it.** Stepping onto ANY staircase in the clearing, from any side, starts the loop: the ten whole flights, the five broken stubs and the original in the middle. A stub or the original hands the player straight on to a whole flight in the fog.
- **The flights.** Nothing is lit until the player chooses. The whole staircases are long (about fifty steps) and all much the same size. From then on the light and the compass follow the flight they are on, and every flight is one-way: once they have started up it they cannot back down, whichever way they face.

1. They climb to the top of the flight they chose. As they reach the landing the fog closes in around them to a near white-out, the view drifts and the hum rises. With no cut, no flash and no stop they are part way up *another* long staircase, facing up it, still walking. The fog thins and the new flight is there in front of them.
2. Sometimes a hand-off puts them at the flight's foot, more often part way up it, a third or half way, so it feels like being stuck on stairs rather than reaching bottoms and tops.
   - There are four to six flights in all (random), each a different one.
   - The voice calls at every hand-off.
   - The blood rain, falling since the cabin door, thickens: a few drops at the first flight, heavy by the last.
   - It should feel fluid and trippy, one long walk, never beats skipped.
3. At the top of the last flight the landing gives way. They stumble to its edge as it sags under them, topple over it (off the back, or over a side wall if the back is blocked) and drop, faster and faster, the view tipping down and slowly rolling: about three seconds in all, never through the stone. The dark comes in over the last of the drop, a soft thump. The blood rain stops with the fall.
4. They wake on the trail past the clearing, looking along it, the same night, in fog so thick the trees are grey. Nothing is said. Behind them the stairs, the ring and the clearing's own flight are simply gone; the meadow stands empty and the trails are as they were. The compass points ahead along the trail, to the cabin.

## Act 7: Safety
**The way to the cabin.** The trail past the clearing comes round through the woods to the cabin, ahead of the player and never back the way they came (no backtracking: the trail is laid out so the cabin is reached going forward, the cabin "in the wrong place" once more). Still night, the rain over. It is a walk of its own. The cabin stands ahead, its window lit and its door open, no rain on the roof.

**Inside.** It is warm and safe: the hearth lit, the axe over the hearth, the assignment sheet and the two-person photo on the table. Stepping in through the door saves the player's place (**the cabin door**, a save within checkpoint 5). At the photo board, the pictures are gone. One print is left, of the stairs.
- **The fire.** Looking at it, the print animates: its edges curl and the stairs in it begin to burn, and the fire jumps from the photo to the wall. Smoke fills the room from the ceiling down and the roof beams groan.
- **Trapped.** Part of the roof caves in over the door, burning beams across the doorway. There is no other way out.
- **The axe.** The axe over the hearth can now be taken (E). With it the player chops through the fallen beams in the doorway (held E, three blows) and breaks out.
- **Too slow.** The fire gives them about forty-five seconds from the cave-in. Past that the smoke closes the view to black: "Burned Alive". Back to the cabin door.

**Outside.** Out of the cabin, they turn to look back at it burning. That is **Checkpoint 6**. The cabin is sealed by the fire from here on.

**Der Riese.** Far beyond the burning cabin, a GIANT version of the stalker walks the one open sky lane behind the far tree line, coming up out of the murk over seconds, never a pop.
- Only its neck and head show, moving through the tops of the far trees, to add scale and dread. It snaps its head and body round in the unnatural way it will at the top of the last staircase.
- **The herding.** From the cabin to the ridge, trees fall where the giant looks, as the walls fell in Act 1 (each creaks first and falls only in the player's view, flat and heavy, never within reach). They close the woods on either side and behind, so the only way is up the ridge to the lookout. It never touches the player.
- At the lookout, high over the creek, the burning cabin is in view below. The giant paces the lane, slow pass after slow pass, until the player has stood at the lookout watching the fire (or gone on past it). Then it makes its last pass and dissolves as it walks on out of the lane.
- Its deep, distant rattle rides with it in bursts, with the soft thud of its stride, felt more than heard.
- It is gone, never seen again until the last staircase.
- The player keeps control the whole time.
- **The herding stops at the lookout.** The walk from the lookout to the bunker is open woods: no trees fall on it, so the second arrow trail can leave it.

**No whispers on the walk.** The walk to the bunker is silent of the voice.

**The bunker.** The compass now points onward to a new structure, a bunker deep in the woods beyond the ridge. About halfway there his second arrow trail leaves the path, in the same ink and among the same kind of decoys, and leads to the cairn that hides the other half of the key. Bare footprints in the ink lead from the lookout all the way to the bunker's door.
- **The approach.** Roots and ivy grow through the vault door's frame. Near it the ambience cuts to silence and a hum rises, as at the stairs. The third chapter's title, *Root of the Problem*, comes up over the vault door.
- **The keyhole.** The door has a brass keyhole in its face, low and right of the wheel. Without both halves it is "Locked. The key's in two pieces somewhere." (with one, "Half a key won't turn it. The other half's still out there."), and a line under the picture says where the missing half's trail starts. With both, using the door brings the two halves up in the hand, pinched between thumb and finger: the bit's knurled sleeve is screwed down onto the other half's threaded stub with a click, and the whole key goes into the lock and turns. The bolts clunk back and the door swings open and stays open.
- Before the clearing's loop is done, the bunker's door stays shut and sends them back: something is still calling across the creek.
- If they reach the bunker with the cabin's save missed, the approach saves **Checkpoint 6** there instead, once the clearing's loop is done.

## Act 8: The Bunker
**Checkpoint 7**, when entering the bunker.

Inside, the player is in a long hallway with overhead fluorescent tubes lining the way forward. The hallway is almost an optical illusion in the way it seems to stretch on forever. Distances are stencilled down its walls, sealed doors stand along it, a phone hangs off its hook, and ivy creeps in over its last stretch.

**The boots.** Just inside stands a pair of boots with nobody in them, R.H.'s initials inked inside their tops: he took them off here and walked on barefoot (the bare footprints outside are his).

**The lights.** The further the player walks down the hallway, the white tubes start to flicker, then eventually transition to a deep, unsettling fluorescent red.
- If the player turns around after the red-light trigger, all the lights behind them back to the entrance change to red too, to add to the feeling of the player's sanity slipping.
- Part way down, once the lamps have begun to fail, they all die at once and the creature is standing right in front of the player. It lunges at their face, and is gone the same instant the lamps snap back red.

**The end.** At the end of the hallway, the clean industrial bunker floor gives way to an overgrown, viny door, shut, waiting for the player to push it open (E to interact) and walk inside.

## Act 9: Belly of the Beast
When the player goes through the overgrown door, they see a room filled with old CRT TVs stacked on top of each other, like that scene in The Matrix, showing night-vision feeds.
- **The woods.** Fixed shots: the trail, the footbridge, the fork's sign, the bunker's own door, the tunnel, the lake shore, and now and then someone tall and dark among the trees.
- **His path.** More screens replay the player's own way in order, as fixed shots of the places he passed (the camp, the cabin boarded, the footbridge, the clearing), and end on the cabin burning.
- **The arrival.** One screen shows the trailhead lot on the day he arrived: the car with its trunk open, and him lifting the camera out of it. He was watched from the start.

The station's log lies open on the console. The moment they are through, the vine door swings shut behind them on its own. It stays locked ("It won't move.") until the screens are done and the dead walkie-talkie is taken off the console: pushing it before then only rattles it.

**The screens.**
1. The compass points to one specific screen showing the cabin burning.
2. An interact prompt appears to turn it off. When the player presses E and turns off that TV, they all turn off.
3. After 5 seconds they all turn back on, showing one image in unison. It starts fuzzy, then slowly clears until it is all the stairs in the woods.

The walkie-talkie lies on the console beside the log, dead: no light, no sound. The player takes it (E); nothing happens. The compass then points to the way out (the bunker's entrance). The way out will not change until the walkie-talkie is in hand.

## Act 10: Escaping the Bunker
With the screens done and the walkie-talkie in hand, the shut door gives: pushing it open again (E, or walking into it), the long straight hallway is gone. They are in a bare concrete room with three doors, and the door they came in by shut behind them.

**The choir.** *"Come and see"* is chanted in unison by a choir of unsteady, wavering voices.

**The rooms.** Open any door and the player is standing in the same room again, the door behind them shut: the same lamp, the same stains, the same three doors. Deep lighting colour changes, the feeling of something following. This repeats a random number of times (two to four).

**It.** Then the room they step into has the creature in it, hanging upside down from the ceiling, facing them. It is the stalker.
- The scare is the old walkie-talkie burst, the super loud static, with its roar under it.
- Then the lamp dies: the room is completely black and all that can be seen are its two glowing eyes. The doors ahead slam shut.
- The door behind the player swings open and they are shoved through it, out into a long hall. The bunker's own round front door is at its far end, with the night beyond; the door slams shut at their back. The only way is down the hall.
- As they run, its clicking rattle grows louder and quicker behind them, and heavy footfalls follow. Glance aside and there is nothing there. But turn back toward the door at all and, once, it is right there in front of their face for a blink, then gone. All the way to the round door.

**The walkie-talkie.** The player bursts out through the bunker's original front entrance. Outside, in the night in front of the hatch, the dead walkie-talkie in their hand crackles to life. That is **Checkpoint 8**.

## Act 11: The Third Man
**The radio.** Once you pick up the radio, a static-y voice begins to ask you questions for the rest of the game.
- *"Did you see them?"* the radio scoffs out.
- *"Who are you!"* the player shouts back: his own voice, dry and unfiltered, not through the radio.
- Static. Radio silence follows.

The compass points onward, deeper into the woods beyond the bunker, to the stairs: the first staircase is standing there again. On the walk the radio asks *"Are you alone out there?"*

**It followed them out.** The stalker from the bunker's rooms comes out of the bunker's door behind them and keeps to their trail.
- It is only ever seen when they turn round. Looked at, it freezes where it stands, its head tipping; looked away from, it comes on faster.
- Near the last staircase it matches their pace. If it gets close it shoves them, hard, spinning them round to face the stairs.
- It cannot climb: at the foot of the flight it stops, to one side, and stands staring up after them, eyes glowing. It is gone once the cap is back on.

**The climb.** This time the stairs are far bigger and go impossibly high up into the trees, their top lost in the fog.
- The player climbs them on their own feet, no pull. The climb is short, about thirty-two seconds, and there is no way back down (a couple of treads up, the way behind is closed).
- The hum rises with every step until it is very loud at the top.
- The stairs themselves say *"come up and see"* only once or twice: once from somewhere up in the fog as the player comes under them, and once about half way up.
- The radio keeps at them the whole way: *"Don't go up."* as they come under the stairs. Then on the climb: *"Who's there with you?"*, *"Don't. Don't go up there."*, *"Who's that behind you?"* and, near the top, *"Is someone with you?"*

**The top.** On the top landing the broken newel post is right there, in reach at last. The cap the player has carried since the cabin goes back on it (E, "Put the cap back"; empty-handed the stump only reads "The broken post."). It leaves their hands, turns, and grinds down onto the stump, stone on stone, with a purple flash at the joint. The hum surges. Only this staircase is ever made whole. From here the player has no control.
1. The treetops are covered in thick fog.
2. The radio asks *"Do you see him now?"*

**The giant.** The hum swells, and forty metres beyond the landing the giant (the one that paced the sky lane in Act 7) rises out of the woods: head first, never smoothly.
- It holds, lurches up metres in a tenth of a second, sinks back, oozes. It comes up stooped and listing, its head upside down on a drawn-out neck.
- Its great glowing red eyes find the player, and it closes in on the top of the stairs.

**The throw.** An animated cutscene; control is taken, and the motion is smooth throughout, with no hard shake and no flashing.
1. Its hand comes down over the top of the stairs and closes round the player: the view held in the black of its fingers, a slit of fog and treetops.
2. It lifts them high over the treetops, the woods falling away below.
3. It throws them. They fly in a long arc, rising and then downward, the trees rushing past at the edges of their view, faster as they fall.
4. Black, the hum cut dead.

Over the black, after a silence, the radio asks one last time: *"Did you see them?"* Then the fourth chapter's title, *Dawn is Breaking*, comes up over the black. **Checkpoint 9.** The throw is the stalker herding him.

## Act 12: The Lake
**Checkpoint 9**, on the lake's dock.

**The wake.** Dawn. He comes to on the ground by the lake. Behind him is the crater where he landed and the long furrow his body dragged through the earth to where he lies. He gets up and turns round, and the dock is revealed: he is standing at the head of the lake's dock, mist on the water.

**The rowboat.** Tied at the dock is a rowboat, its rope knotted in a hurry, tight and ugly (nobody says by whom: it was the stalker). The player's line: *"Dad's rowboat?"* He remembers his father's station across this lake and rowing out to it as a boy. His father was a forest ranger, and the station across the lake was his main headquarters, which he ran.

**The crossing.**
- W rows, both oars pulling together on their own rhythm; Shift rows hard, for as long as the breath lasts; A and D steer (or, alternated, row the old way).
- The fog closes in on the water as they go, and thins again over the last stretch so the station comes out of it.
- Something passes under the boat and bumps the hull. A foghorn sounds far off. A limb covered in bloodshot eyes comes up out of the fog to watch them pass, and slides back under. Something knocks on the planks from below.
- Then the hush: a shadow slides under the boat and the water stirs, while they are still rowing.
- Short of halfway, with no warning, it bursts out of the water ahead of them: the boat is thrown back and stopped, spray over the bow (no eye opening first). Limbs covered in bloodshot eyes breach the water all round, a foghorn blast under them, and the lake turns white-capped. A hundred eyes open and blink together. One limb arches over the boat, looking down into it.

**The hunt.** From then on a limb hunts the boat.
- Row steadily and it falls behind, but every so often it comes for them and only rowing hard holds it off.
- Limbs surface closer and closer, and two rear up and slam down at the boat: row hard or steer away.
- Every crossing has one near miss, scripted, after the first strike: something surfaces right behind the boat, close enough to touch, rears there a moment and goes under again.
- Over the last stretch it gives chase all the way to the beach.
- Let it close and it knocks on the hull and rears up behind the stern; dawdle and it takes the boat and drags the player under. "The lake took you." reloads the shore.

The crossing is one stretch, with no save in the middle.

**The far shore.** The far shore holds a dilapidated forest rescue station behind a shut plank door; E swings it out onto the lobby. One of R.H.'s torn pages lies on the near shore.

## Act 13: The Forester Station
**Checkpoint 10**, at the station's door.

This is his father's headquarters. The station shows a man running from something, and something that kept writing after he stopped. A puzzle stretch through a station that starts kept and tidy and is left more wrecked with every room solved. The lobby decays while the player is out of it: stained, rotten, then industrial, then flesh.
- **The desk.** On the lobby desk sits the photo the player saw in the Act 1 cabin, the man and the boy, aged and handled soft, the man's corner rubbed nearly smooth. Beside it a nameplate: M. WEBBER, CHIEF RANGER. Looking at the photo brings the player's line *"That's... mine."*
- **Barricades.** The windows are boarded from the inside, furniture is stacked at the doors, and a half-packed duffel sits by the exit.
- **The lobby changes.** As well as the decay, on each return the nameplate is turned a little further, or a drawer is open that was not. Nothing explains it (it is the stalker, not the father). The first time the player comes back into the lobby and finds it changed, the player's line *"Dad... what happened here?"*
- **The notice.** Pinned on the lobby wall, a typed notice dated February 1988: the relief crew reached the station and found no one, and the three rangers, Travis Alexander, Nathan Daniels and Mitchell Webber, are declared dead.
- **The lobby's knife.** A knife is stuck in the lobby desk. The basement door is duct-taped shut (by his father), and the knife cuts it open.
- **The basement.** The lights sputter and die. The flooded basement is drained with a valve wheel, and a dead eye swirls into the grate. The grandfather clock (its hands set to six, the start of his father's shift) gives its drowned chime and spits out a key. The key is his: on his key rack by the desk, the hook marked CLOCK is empty.
- **Room 1.** It has red writing on its walls (DO NOT LOOK AT THEM / DO NOT TOUCH THEM / NEVER GO UP THEM) and a taped cigar box. In the box are two men's things, a watch and a ring (Travis's and Nathan's). Under the tape is a button, a coat button off a ranger's coat. Pressed, it sends the words dripping down the walls into pools of blood, and opens Room 2 (**Checkpoint 12**).
- **The logbook.** Room 1 holds his father's logbook, open on a table: twelve dated entries from November 30th, 1987 to January 4th, 1988. The first nine, to January 1st, are his; the last three (January 2nd to 4th) are in a steadier hand and a different ink, copying and overdoing his slipping spelling. After January 4th every page reads "Come and see." over and over, drifting, doubling, indenting and crowding the margins like the pages in The Shining. The full text is in the appendix. It reads in the note overlay, page by page.
- **The chained door.** In the lobby's back wall, right of the web, a narrow door is chained shut from the outside, a footlocker, a crate and a chair stacked against it (the room in the December 31st entry). It stays chained until Room 3.
- **Room 2.** A brass cryptex sits over the plaque "WHAT YOU WERE TOLD NEVER TO GO UP", with his father's handwriting along its edge: it is what he told the player as a child.
  - The door slams behind them, and someone starts knocking softly.
  - The first touch of the box brings one of the lake's limbs through the window. The lake pours in and the room fills to the ceiling, the water turning to blood.
  - The word is STAIRS. Solved, the flood is drawn back out and the box gives up an old lighter.
  - Too slow: "You drowned." It reloads at Room 1's button.
- **Behind the desk.** The lighter burns the web away from the door behind the desk. It shows an iron door with three hollows: LOOK, TOUCH, CLIMB. Pencilled beside each hollow, in his father's hand, is a rule: *don't look when it looks*, *don't touch what's cold*, *don't climb*.
  - **LOOK:** the dead eye now moves round the dark basement, but only while nobody is looking.
  - **TOUCH:** the pale hand lies in the pool under DO NOT TOUCH THEM. In the others, something takes the player's wrist.
  - **CLIMB:** in Room 2 a short staircase now goes up to nothing; at the top it goes dark, something knocks behind them, and they come to holding one of its steps.
- **The iron door.** The eye, the hand and the step set in their places open it, onto Room 3.
- **The locked room.** Once Room 3 is reached, the chain on that door hangs cut, the stack is shoved aside and the door stands ajar. It is optional. Inside is the room his father locked: a bare cot and a blanket, no body, and every wall written over in blood, "Come and see.", in his father's handwriting, up to the ceiling. Nothing says what became of the man locked inside.

The stairs are never explained.

## Act 14: The Stairwell
**Checkpoint 13**, through the iron door into Room 3: riveted steel, 42 portraits with their faces burnt out. A few survive as ranger staff photographs with the faces scratched out; no one says who did it. The compass dies here and hunts on its own until the church's great door.

**The descent.** A trench in the floor leads down into the longest descent in the game: square steel stairs round an open well, twenty turns, nearly black. The walls go from clean concrete to oily grime as it goes. Its own music comes in partway down, timed so its build lands near the bottom, and fades as they arrive. Three minutes of pure dread, with nothing chasing until the crawler.
- **The numbers.** The numbers on the landings go wrong: they stick, count back, turn to tallies, and read TURN BACK, YOU WERE TOLD, NEVER.
- **Other things on the way down:**
  - footsteps across the well, keeping time;
  - a door marked 3 that someone knocks on from the other side;
  - a chair falling down the well that never lands;
  - a figure at the rail below, gone when they get there;
  - a long silence near the bottom.
- **The flame dies.** A quarter of the way down, the lantern's flame gutters out and won't take again ("The flame won't take. The wick only smoulders. B: the blacklight still works."). From here to the top of the round room the wick only smoulders: a weak, guttering red ember-glow a few steps round them, and the blacklight is the only real light, switched to with B for what is written in its ink (a hint comes once in each place when they stand by such writing with only the ember showing). The lantern does not switch itself to the blacklight here.

**The crawler.** A turn after the flame dies, a pale, starved thing on all fours lets itself down onto the stairs above them. It is a mask of the stalker.
- It hangs back, surges closer and falls back again, never far behind however fast they go. If they stop, it comes on. Running is the way to keep ahead of it.
- Running down the stairs is just running: nothing takes them back up the shaft but the crawler.
- If it reaches them, it lunges, black, and they come to three turns further up with it above them again.

**The bottom.** The next flight has fallen away. At the edge: **Jump across?** or **Jump down?**
- Across, the far landing holds a moment and then shears off the wall. They fall, and lie out cold in the black with a slow heartbeat before they come to among the wreckage.
- Down, they land on their feet.

The crawler watches from above, and is gone when they jump. Either way they are in a chamber under the stairwell (**Checkpoint 14**).

## Act 15: The Long Hallway
**The narrow hallway.** A red-lit concrete corridor a body's width across and lost in the dark overhead, about a hundred and twenty metres of it. Then a taller hall with green lamps on long cables, about three hundred and thirty metres long. The stalker stands in it, in its own true form (it replaced the shadow man): the same black, rag-hung thing from the woods, its two ember eyes, frozen in the green, caught in a new pose wherever it is in the red, its head following the eye. Walking past it the first time saves the player's place.

**Red light, green light.** The lamps flicker, go red with a clunk and a low siren, and stay red a few seconds.
- In the first half it appears behind the player and jumps closer, its rattle each time.
- In the second half it is in front of them, coming at them in one of several ways: an inch at a time with its eyes and rattle growing, in lunges from far down the hall, standing still until the red's last moment, looming taller, its eyes melting, leaning out round the wall's edge as it does round a trunk, or three of it.
- Moving in the red means being taken: it throws its arms at them, and "It Taketh". The player wakes just past where it first stood.

**The end.** At the end, a janitor's closet; the door swings shut behind them (**Checkpoint 15**).

## Act 16: The Closet
Pitch black. The door won't open in the dark: the switch by the door brings the light up through red and green to an ordinary white. The far door then opens onto the sewer's landing beyond, seen through it, and stepping through carries the player onto the landing with no cut. A short, sharp spike.

## Act 17: The Sewer
**Checkpoint 16**, on the sewer's landing inside a steel door. One of R.H.'s pages lies on the landing.

A long round brick pipe, ankle-deep and dragging at every step, opens into a vast flooded cistern of brick vaults. In the middle stands a platform with a square black hole, smoke rising out of it. "Drop down into the hole?" It takes two yeses.
- The first no: "come and see", whispered from one pipe mouth, then another, then another, round the room.
- The second: the lamps brown out, dim and orange and stuttering, and die one by one; a brown murk comes up that makes the room hard to see across, and the whispers multiply and don't stop.
- After that, each no only brings them closer.

Hauntings are spaced 16 to 34 seconds apart, so this reads as a wet, uneasy crossing, not another spike.

## Act 18: The Pit
**Checkpoint 17**, on landing. A great square pit ringed by a catwalk, a sea of blood five feet below it, and the thing from the lake in it. Amber work lamps in wire cages light the catwalk.

**The valves.** "The valves. Turn them. Drain it." Old carbon-arc searchlights, one on each side, throw a hard warm beam through the dust onto ten of sixteen valves, one at a time. Turning them drains the blood and rots the beast, from glossy hide to a zombified ruin.

**The slams.** Its limbs slam the catwalk under red circles: reared overhead, swept across, corkscrewing down, hammered twice, cracked like a whip. They come more often, faster and wider, and in barrages as it goes.
- Only the circle kills: "It crushed you." That's back to the landing.
- The catwalk is dented and its rails bent where they land, and it stays battered after a death.

**The end.** With the last valve the pit empties and its 94 eyes burst, slowly at first and faster and faster. A steel door rises across the pit onto warm light (**Checkpoint 18**).

## Act 19: The Library
Chapter five: *Ever Upward*.

The calmest place in the game: a warm library, a real fire, a ticking clock.
- **The puzzle box.** A sheet over a side table hides a bamboo puzzle box. Solved, it burns away and leaves a red silk bookmark.
- **The book.** One book sticks out a finger's width; only the blacklight shows a palm print on its spine. The bookmark slid into it opens the bookcase on a candle-lit stone passage.
- **The journal.** Found by the blacklight, a journal on the shelves holds a riddle: "the lefts are feeling down, the rights are all right, and if that's true the middles are doing just fine. Both walls say the same thing."

## Act 20: The Round Room
**Checkpoint 19**, stepping in. A tall round stone room, its windows bricked up, old web all over the floor and slung over a dais in the middle. Bare bulbs on a conduit light it, and a ring round the dais glows red.

**The webs.** The lighter burns the web away, out across the floor and up the walls.

**The switches.** Six old knife switches hang three to each side wall. Set as the journal says (the left down, the middle to the middle, the right up), they bring the power back with a surge:
- the bulbs swell and burst one by one;
- every candle in the drum lights itself;
- green runs round the ring until it is solid.

Before that the dais does nothing: "Nothing. The ring stays red."

**The ascent.** The dais rises up a shaft into a small room above (**Checkpoint 20**). Chapter six: *The Great Door*. "Up. Always up, from here." The lantern's flame takes again.

## Act 21: The Church
**The long stair.** A long straight stair of 16 flights climbs to the surface, concrete giving way to old timber. At the top a hatch opens into a church's crypt (**Checkpoint 21**); it drops shut behind them and won't lift again. It is winter everywhere now. One of R.H.'s pages lies in the crypt.

**The church.** The church is a Gothic cathedral standing alone in a snowy glade. The compass is still dead in here.
- **The candles.** Four candles are to be lit with the lighter. When all four burn, a door opens across the church.
- **The vestry.** The vestry holds the font's key and the sexton's note: he put the four away, the cup in the font, the bell where bells go, the crown with the dead, the dove to the Lady. "The house listens to fire."
- **The door.** The font opened gives a silver chalice. Set in the great door's niche, the gold shapes in the other three niches take its light one by one and the door opens on the snow (**Checkpoint 22**).

## Act 22: The Winter Woods
From **Checkpoint 22**, outside the great door. The compass lives again and points down a plowed road through the winter woods to a ski lodge. Halfway down the road saves the player's place. Far off, a church bell tolls a slow lullaby.

**The wendigo** stalks the woods. It is the stalker's mask, and it wears Mitchell (see "Lore").
- The first time, it stands in the middle of the road ahead; looked at, it is gone.
- After that it is behind them: by a tree, crouched on top of the plowed bank, dropping down onto the road, caught half way up a bare trunk. It is glimpsed only when the player turns round, and gone up into the trees the instant they look near it, the boughs dumping their snow behind it.
- It never touches them.
- It speaks in voices it has copied. Once, among them, is a voice he almost knows: his father's, borrowed.

**The lodge.** The lodge's front doors are chained from inside ("Chained. From the inside."). The back door is iced into its frame and forced open on the mudroom (**Checkpoint 23**).

## Act 23: The Ski Lodge
Behind them the roof lets its snow go and buries the back door: snowed in. Chapter seven: *Enjoy Your Stay*. Every lamp is lit, Christmas is up, and nobody is here. (The guests were snowed in since Christmas with the decorations still up; the wendigo found them and killed them one by one, luring each.) Things change behind their back: a chair turned to face them, wet footprints from the front doors to where they stood. A music box upstairs winds down the lullaby.

- **The bar:** room 202's keycard is taped under the counter's lip, found only by the blacklight. The closing staff's note is on its back.
- **Room 202:** a key in a note in one of its drawers (a different one each game): the servants' pantry. Leaving, 202's door slams and jams (**Checkpoint 24**); behind it something says it's cold. Starving.
- **The wardrobes:** 202's and 204's can be stepped into and pulled to. The first time, something comes up the corridor, stops outside the door, breathes, and goes on.
- **The pantry:** a mop falls across the door as it opens; 203's card is in a drawer.
- **Room 203:** the window is open, the room full of snow, the dining room's key on the sill. Through a hole in the bathroom wall are 204 and a smudged note ("If you can hear it, it already knows you are here."). 203 slams and jams too (**Checkpoint 25**). R.H.'s last page lies in the corridor: *"It wears the ones it takes. It wore my voice at the lodge door and I nearly let myself in. If you hear me after this, it isn't me."*
- **The dining hall:** six long tables under white sheets. Among the dust sheets one stands a person's height, and it is nearer every time their light goes out. Under the tables, in the order they're pulled:
  1. dishes left for weeks;
  2. a smear of blood end to end, and laughter outside;
  3. a headless frozen skeleton, and the storm gets up;
  4. roaches pouring off a wendigo's skull on a silver platter, bleeding, and the laughter all round the room. The skull turns to follow them;
  5. the fifth table breaks and comes down;
  6. always last, 201's keycard standing in a bowl of snow, which melts into blood as it's taken (**Checkpoint 26**).
- **Room 201:** the one perfect room. An envelope: "Welcome Back. We missed you as a valued guest." The door slams for good (**Checkpoint 27**). In the bathroom a red light shows a hole hacked through into the walls.
- **The crawlspace:** a maze between the walls, pine boards and old brick, pipes across at head height to duck under.
  - It rots as they go, and missing posters and old newspapers are pinned along it. One is R.H.'s, his name torn off and "the steps" pencilled over it.
  - Something howls in the walls the whole way. Halfway is a save (**Checkpoint 28**).
  - Where the Christmas lights begin along the ceiling, the lantern dies; the lights are all there is until they are out.
  - Its arm comes through the boards four times, feeling for them, reaching and grabbing, and the only way on is under it. A tin thrown past it draws it off.
  - At the end, the back of a wardrobe: pushed over, it's the dining hall, and while they were in the walls the lodge has frozen over (**Checkpoint 29**).
- **The front doors:** chained ("Requires Master Key.", though there is no master key) until the freeze; now they stand ajar, and give a little at a time against the snow.
  1. A howl behind: it's on the balcony across the hall. "STARVING..... FREEZING..... FOREVER...." (the same words as the log's last true pages, which it wrote).
  2. It climbs onto the rail and crouches there, then comes off it at them. They throw themselves aside and it lands where they stood, down on its hands on the boards, a few steps away.
  3. It turns and looks at them, rises over them, and leans down into their face.
  4. They thrust the lantern up at it. It flinches from the flame, then strikes the lantern out of their hand. The lantern bursts against the foot of the Christmas tree, and the tree goes up; the fire spreads through the hall.
  5. **What it wears.** In the firelight, steady (no flicker strobing), the player sees it: Mitchell's ranger badge on its chest, and a name plate like the one on his father's desk. The player's line: *"Dad?"* It answers in his father's borrowed voice, the voice that called him up the stairs in Act 1: *"Come and see."*
  6. It turns to the doors and strikes them once (they bow and hold, and its claws rake down through them), draws back, and goes through them: one leaf wrenched off its hinge, the other flung out across the porch.
  7. Outside, the storm has buried the front of the lodge in a tunnel of snow, and it is gone down it.
  8. They get up, look back at the hall going up in flames, walk to the splintered doorway, and look out, left, and right (**Checkpoint 30**).

The fire turns them back from the hall from here on. The lantern stays out until the end of the hunt ("The glass is cracked. It won't light.").

## Act 24: The Snow Maze
**The way down.** Out of the doorway it's a vault of packed snow, and at its end, low in a wall of snow, a black hole.
- A few metres in, the wendigo is in the doorway behind them. It rears and screams, and drives them down the tunnel, coming on faster if they dawdle or turn back.
- Caught: "It drove you down. You were too slow." That's back to the doorway.

**The maze.** Down the hole is a maze of tunnels in the snow far below (**Checkpoint 31**, "So quiet down here."): blue ice scalloped like the inside of a shell, icicles in curtains, a pale shaft of daylight here and there down a hole in the roof, and very quiet. There are dead ends, loops that bring them back round, and one right way through. Green road flares stand in the snow along it: taken, a flare lights the way a little while; thrown, it lands burning. Their boots and its claws leave prints that stay.

The wendigo follows them down, hunting.
- **It hears:** running carries far along the tunnels, walking less, crouching hardly at all, and standing still not at all. Behind it, it hears half as well.
- **It sees** in a cone ahead of it, out into the dark, but not through the snow. Crouching keeps them harder to see; a flare in the hand lets it see them from further off.
- **It goes to a thrown flare** and crouches over it while it burns. It learns: if it sees the throw it comes for them, and later it falls for only some. A flare landing near it mid-chase blinds it for a moment.
- **Hollows** under low drifts at dead ends hide them, crouched. It won't go in, but it sniffs at the mouth: held breath (V) and it moves on; breathe, and it hears.
- **A director** above it always knows where they are and never tells it. If they've been safe too long it nudges the wendigo their way. If it has been on them too long, it calls it off somewhere else.
- **Caught:** "It found you."

**The trenches.** At the maze's middle is a great cavern, and in it trenches from a war eighty years gone (**Checkpoint 32**, "Trenches."): shored with posts and planks, sandbags, helmets and rifles, soldiers still in their gear, crates. One crate is stencilled "FLAME THROWER, PORTABLE" and nailed shut. A crowbar lies somewhere in the trenches. Prised open, the crate holds a flamethrower: "A flamethrower. It still works." (**Checkpoint 33**). Far off in the tunnels, it screams: it heard.
- **The flamethrower** is their only weapon. Its fuel never runs out, but it overheats: held too long, it locks and vents for seven seconds. Its fire is a rope of burning napalm that arcs out, splashes and clings where it lands, and rolls up into black smoke.
- **The hunt turns round:** burned, the wendigo becomes the prey. It runs to a dead end far off and waits there, crouched, its breathing, its smouldering burns and its claw marks leading to it, and it comes out at them when they're close. It can still kill.
- **Four burns** and it's finished (**Checkpoint 34**).

The song ("Petty Theft") carries the whole act, rising as it nears. Its synths sound for finds, notes and solved puzzles all through the game; at the saves they are heard only from this act on.

## Act 25: Return
**The reveal.** Burning, the wendigo falls against the maze's wall and burns through it: a way out. Then the mask burns off it. Out of the falling, burning shape the gaunt black figure from Act 4 stands up, unburned in the flames, and watches the player. Then it steps back into the snow and is gone. It cannot be killed.
- Where the mask fell, Mitchell's ranger badge lies in the ash. E takes it, and the player's line: *"You can rest now, Dad."*
- **The photos after.** From this moment, every picture the player takes develops with the black figure standing somewhere behind the subject, tall and black with only the faint pale plane of its face, at the edge of the frame. It is never in the world, only in the prints.

Chapter eight: *Return*. They come out of a hole in a snowbank, the snow thinning into the autumn woods it all began in. The lantern lights again. Down the trail they go past the cabin from Act 1, burned out from the fire in Act 7: its door is shut, and E pushes it in on a cold, dark room. Inside, the board is charred and the assignment sheet is ash on the table. Then on to the trailhead's lot and the car.

Which of five endings they see is decided by how many pictures they took, how many times they died, and how long they took:
- **1** (more than ten pictures, one to ten deaths, over two hours): the camera back in the trunk. Black. The credits.
- **2** (every picture, no deaths, over two hours): the camera back in the trunk. Black, held five seconds; far off, it howls. The credits.
- **3** (ten deaths or more, fewer than ten pictures, over two hours): the wendigo crashes down on the car and comes at them burning. They turn, the burning mask drops away, and the stalker stands behind it. Black. The credits.
- **4** (more than ten pictures, one death, under two hours): fog. Where the car should be are the stairs, blood running down them a step at a time. They climb, not by choice, faster and faster. Black. The credits.
- **5** (every picture, no deaths, under two hours): fog, but the car really is there. The camera in the trunk; black, five seconds; then they're in the driver's seat, and the engine turns over. The credits.

A record off the table falls to the nearest ending.

## The end
After the last act come:
1. The save that marks the story done (**GameFinished**), and the fade.
2. The end card, DEAD SILENT.
3. The player's pictures played back as Polaroids, the best of them gathered last, and how many things they photographed and their points ("N of 70", "N points"). Every one of them, taken before the reveal or after, develops in the slideshow with the black figure standing behind it.
4. "GLHFDD", "Thanks for playing.", and the main menu.

A Continue afterwards finds the player where the story ended, by the car.

## Lore (team only)
None of this is said outright in the game. It is what the game shows, for the team to keep straight.

**The people.**
- **Cameron Webber**, the player: a young photographer. In late October 1996 he goes back to woods he loved as a child, to take nature shots for a magazine's October feature (*[magazine TBD]: Autumnal Delights*). He shoots with a Polaroid-style instant camera, and his editor reaches him by pager. He believes his father died here, and he is right. He is not looking for him.
- **Mitchell Webber**, his father: chief ranger, and the forester station across the lake was his headquarters. The rowboat at the dock is his. He died at the station after his last true log entry, on January 1st, 1988; how is never shown.
- **Travis Alexander and Nathan Daniels**, the two rangers snowed in with him. Travis followed a blood trail into the woods on December 28th and was not found. Nathan died on the night of December 30th and was locked in a room; there is no body there now.
- **R.H.**, the stranger: a hiker the stairs took a day or two before Cameron. He tries to get whoever comes after him safely through. Never seen, never worn.

**Timeline.**
- Winter 1987 to 1988: Mitchell and the two rangers are snowed in at the station. The relief never comes. Mitchell's last true entry is January 1st, 1988; the stalker writes the rest, to January 4th, and every page after. In February 1988 the relief crew finds no one, and the three are declared dead.
- The ski lodge: snowed in over a Christmas, its decorations still up into January, as lodges leave them. The wendigo found it and killed the guests one by one, luring each.
- Cameron then was a child or young teen; he rowed out to the station on the lake as a boy.
- Late October 1996: the game.

**The stalker.** One enemy in many masks. It cannot be killed.
- **True form:** the gaunt, hunched black figure of Act 4 (and of the bunker, Act 11's follower and Act 15's hall).
- **At giant size:** the giants of Acts 7 and 11 are the stalker itself, stooped and listing, head upside down on a drawn-out neck, great red eyes, rising in stop-start lurches. The throw in Act 11 is it herding him.
- **Masks:** the limb of eyes (the lake, Act 12, and the pit, Act 18), the crawler (Act 14), and the wendigo (Acts 22 to 25).
- **The wendigo is the stalker wearing Mitchell:** his badge, his name plate, his voice and his handwriting. It is not Mitchell, who is dead. The log's last pages say starving and cold; the wendigo says STARVING, FREEZING, FOREVER. Same author.
- **It never wears R.H.**
- **The stairs** are its gateway into our world, and climbing them in Act 1 invited it in. This is never said in the game; the stairs stay unexplained.
- **Tells, on every mask:** it moves only when unobserved; it makes no sound of its own beyond its clicking rattle and its footsteps; it shows as a tall black silhouette with only a faint pale face.

**How it looks** (as built).
- **Size and shape.** About 2.4 m, gaunt and hunched, with heavy high shoulders crowned with knotted roots. A small long head slung forward and low on a thin neck. Arms past the knees to long many-jointed fingers with dark hooked nails. Knees bent a little wrong, four long toes.
- **Face.** A small stretched mask of a face, too small for the skull and pressed flat: two deep sockets with the eyes far back, a lipless slit of a mouth with the corners dragged down and needle teeth, two slits for a nose, stains running down from the eyes and mouth.
- **Jaw.** Torn loose on its left and hanging from its right hinge, on every stalker. It lags, swings and chatters.
- **Hide.** Cracked, leathery and bark-like, with ribs, the spine's knuckles, collarbones and hips pressing through. 45 torn rag strips from the shoulders, arms and hips, and bark shards down the spine and off the elbows.
- **Colour and light.** True black and unlit, a hole in the scene, fogged like everything else. Only the faint pale plane of the face reads. Up close, a very faint cold edge light traces its outline. The eyes show a faint sickly yellow only when the lantern catches them; the giant's eyes are red.
- **Sound and pacing.** Its only voice is the clicking rattle, in many short phrases (ticks, runs, long runs, stutters), never a growl. It has its own audio bus, duller when it is behind the player. A director paces it in three phases: build (it stands at its trees, short spells of steps), peak (rattle episodes, full spells of steps, the stop beat when the player stops), and relax (nothing of it at all, so the woods come back). Hunted, out of the bunker, the relax is short but never nothing.

## Appendix: the father's logbook
Handwritten, twelve entries. The first are clean, and the last four slip more each time. January 2nd to 4th are the stalker's, in a steadier hand and a different ink.

**November 30th, 1987.** The snow is coming down hard again. I sent Travis to gather as much firewood as he can. We will be snowed in soon. Nathan is bringing the rest of the food reserves. I will set the snares for some small game. The well has already frozen over, so we will need more buckets and an axe to break through the ice on the lake.

**December 11th, 1987.** We have been snowed in for 10 days now. Food reserves are starting to run low, but the snares are catching a few rabbits. Travis and Nathan are trying to fight their boredom by playing cards, but I can see they are getting a little stir crazy. Our relief team should come to switch with us in a week. Until then we will keep what we can plowed and keep our eyes open for anyone lost out here.

**December 23rd, 1987.** They didn't come.

**December 25th, 1987.** Well, merry fucking Christmas to us. Our firewood is just about gone and we are drying out frozen sticks and logs we found in the woods. The snares aren't enough to sustain us anymore. The food storage is all but gone. We are going to have to survive on pemmican and water until relief arrives. Travis won't talk to us anymore. He has been wrapped in a blanket staring at the fire for days.

**December 26th, 1987.** We are getting too weak. We can barely break through the ice anymore to get water. The firewood is spent. We are huddling for warmth at night. Nathan said he heard something in the woods when he checked the snares. He said it sounded like a child asking him to come look at something. I'm trying to keep everyone sane, but Travis hasn't spoken to us in days.

**December 28th, 1987.** Travis is gone. Nathan said he found a half-eaten rabbit in one of the snares and Travis followed the blood trail. It was fresh. Unfortunately, after searching for Travis we found nothing. The rabbit in the snare was gone.

**December 29th, 1987.** I heard the voises in the woods.

**December 31st, 1987.** Nathan died last night. I had to put him into one of the rooms and lock the door. I cant bury him now. Im locking all the doors. I have to get out of here, the food and water are gone.

**January 1st, 1988.** An eye.... in the lake.... watched me. The basment pipes broke and it is floding.

**January 2nd, 1988.** Im starveing.

**January 3rd, 1988.** So.. hungrey.. so.. coldd.

**January 4th, 1988.** Come and see.

*[Every remaining page (a few with typos, e.g. "Come and se e.", "Comeand see."): "Come and see." written over and over like the Shining pages, the lines drifting, doubling, indenting and crowding the margins, no date, no signature.]*
